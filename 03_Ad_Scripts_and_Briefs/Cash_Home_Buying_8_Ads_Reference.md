# Cash Home Buying – 8 Ads Reference

**What this is:** the breakdown of the 6 reference videos, what each one gets wrong, and a blueprint for 8 better ads.
**Made:** 2026-09-22. Every number in Part 1 was measured off the downloaded files (yt-dlp, ffprobe, ffmpeg scene detect at 0.3, ffmpeg loudnorm, local faster-whisper "small").
**Pairs with:** `04_Audience_Research/Research_Document_Cash_Home_Buying.md` (personas, pains, objections). Read both.

### Ref 1 – "Why are you still paying the mortgage for a house you want to get rid of?"
- **Works:** Hook names the pain in the first line. Fast cutting (2.13 s). Bold all-caps captions with one colored keyword plus an emoji. CTA explains the next step ("fill out a quick short form").
- **Wrong:**
  - The b-roll does not match the words: tattooed hands caulking a floor on "why are you still paying", a woman at a Bank of America ATM, random people on phones. It reads as a stock montage.
  - Fake scarcity: "We cap how many homes we buy each month and we're getting close… now or never." The research says this audience already expects a scam. Hype confirms it.
  - Vague promises: "100% cash", "I'll add a bonus on top" (what bonus?).
  - 51 s is too long for the message. 228 wpm is rushed.

### Ref 2 – Claymation, "Nobody told you it would feel like this."
- **Works:** Best tone of the six. It names the scam fear ("You've figured it was a trap. Not all of them are.") and ends soft ("It costs nothing to ask"). Same clay character in every shot. "A closing date you pick" matches a desired outcome in the research. The Facebook caption shows the offer math (realtor path vs cash path).
- **Wrong:**
  - Captions are small, thin white text on a cream background. Hard to read with sound off.
  - Hook is vague. "Nobody told you" does not say who this is for.
  - A paper reading "$450,000" appears on "a fair price for it". A random number on a run-down clay house invites the "lowball" doubt it is trying to calm.
  - The best part (the math) is only in the post caption, not in the video.
  - No brand or form shown at the end. Ends on a static house.

### Ref 3 and Ref 5 – "Have you seen all these ads saying they'll buy your property above market value? That's all a lie."
- **Works:** Contrarian hook that attacks the category. Founder on a selfie camera feels real. The same script was cut twice with different b-roll openings (demolition floor vs a man falling off a wheelbarrow ramp). That is a visual hook test, and #3 (2,342,271) has more views than #5 (1,019,339).
- **Wrong:**
  - "They'll give you an offer below half the value of your house, guaranteed" is a claim about competitors nobody can prove.
  - "We'll buy your property at valued price" says nothing.
  - The fail footage opening has nothing to do with selling a house.
  - "Secure your cash offer before it's too late" is fake urgency.
  - Never says how the offer is worked out, so the "lie" callout has no proof behind it.

### Ref 4 – Diego.exits format list (not a home-buyer ad)
The format menu, in the order read out: thirst trap, Zack D Films, Asian doctor, claymation, fight night, talking object, whiteboard, Rick and Morty, skeleton story, Muppet stories, singing ingredients, hyper motion, anime, Disney, low poly, crochet, comic, paper, horror, Lego.
Use it only to choose formats. Several are other people's IP (Rick and Morty, Disney, Lego, Muppets). Do not use those for a paid ad.

### Ref 6 – "Fire your realtor! I did, and it changed my life."
- **Works:** Strongest hook text on screen (big yellow serif "FIRE YOUR REALTOR." over a color-graded shot). First cut at 1.38 s. Shows the real form on a phone ("How soon would you like to sell?"), so the viewer sees the next step. Good length (24.38 s).
- **Wrong:**
  - An actor speaking as a past customer. FTC 16 CFR 465.2 and 255.1 bar testimonials from people who did not have the experience.
  - Cash fanning, cash on a counter, a bill-counting machine. Money shots look like the scam ads the audience fears.
  - "Changed my life" is generic. No persona, no real situation.

### What the refs have in common
1. Every home-buyer ref lists the same features: no repairs, no fees, no showings, no inspections. Nobody owns one clear idea.
2. None shows how the offer is worked out, the one thing the research says skeptics want.
3. Four of the five use hype or urgency. Only #2 sounds like a neighbor.
4. None speaks to one persona. They talk to "anyone who wants to sell."

## Part 2 – Rules that make ours better

Every one of the 8 ads follows these.

**Hook (0–3 s)**
- Name one person and one situation in the first 5 words ("You inherited Mom's house…", not "Want to sell fast?").
- A visual change before 2.5 s. The refs' first cut is between 1.38 s and 3.57 s.
- Hook text on screen, big, in the top third.

**Script**
- 25–35 s, 65–95 spoken words (a sung ad needs fewer, because singing is slower). The refs run 182–228 wpm; aim for about 170 so it doesn't sound rushed. Scripts below: 67–85 words, Ad 5 lyrics 42 words.
- One persona, one pain, one promise per ad. Don't list every feature.
- Name the doubt out loud ("Yes, we're one of those cash buyers").
- Show how the offer works: *market value, minus repairs, minus our costs and profit*. The research says skeptics want this.
- Plain, 7th-grade words. No em dashes. No "top dollar", "best price guaranteed", "life-changing".

**Visuals**
- Every shot shows what the line says. If the words are "the roof leaks", we see the roof leak.
- A new shot about every 2 s (refs: 1.89–3.23 s).
- The same character in every shot of an ad (ref 2 got this right).
- Last shot: the real form on a phone, plus the brand name. Ref 6 is the only one that does this.
- No cash piles, no money counters, no suitcases of cash.

**Captions**
- Big, bold, high contrast, 1–4 words per card, one keyword in color. Refs 1, 3 and 5 are the model. Ref 2 is what not to do.
- Keep them inside the safe zone, clear of the Reels UI at the bottom and right edge.

**Delivery**
- 1080x1920, 30 fps, -14 LUFS (the refs measured -14.2 to -15.1).
- Voiceover over a quiet music bed. Music ducked under the voice.

**Truth and compliance**
- No on-screen number (days to close, hours to an offer, prices) unless it is true for your business. Write your real numbers into the `[ ]` slots below.
- No fake scarcity, no "before it's too late".
- No actor or AI character speaking as a real past customer. Animated characters are shown as a story, not as testimonials.
- No claims about competitors you can't prove.
- Not checked live this session: Meta's Housing Special Ad Category rules and Fair Housing ad rules. Check them before launch.

### Ad 1 – Claymation – "Six hours away"
Fixes ref 2: concrete hook, big captions, math on screen, form at the end.
- **Hook (on screen):** YOU INHERITED MOM'S HOUSE
- **Script:**
  > You inherited Mom's house. And it's six hours away.
  > Every month there's a tax bill. An insurance bill. Another drive back for something small.
  > You've thought about fixing it up. You don't have the time. Or the heart.
  > We buy houses like this one as they are. Her furniture too.
  > We show you how we got our number: what it's worth, minus repairs, minus our costs.
  > You pick the closing date. You never have to make that drive again.
  > Tap below. It's free to ask.
- **Shots:** clay woman in her own kitchen looking at a photo of the clay house → a road map with a long line → bills sliding under the clay house's door → her in the car at night → the clay house full of old furniture → a clay whiteboard: *value − repairs − our costs = your offer* → a calendar, she circles a date → she hands over keys and drives away in daylight → phone with the form + brand.

### Ad 2 – Talking Object – "I'm the house"
- **Hook:** the house's front door and windows form a face. It sighs. On screen: I'M THE HOUSE YOU DON'T WANT
- **Script:**
  > Hi. I'm the house you don't want.
  > I know. I have a leaky roof, a weird smell in the basement, and a yard nobody's mowed since spring.
  > And I still cost you money every month.
  > Here's the thing. You don't have to fix me.
  > [Your Company] buys houses like me as-is. No clean-out. No showings.
  > They'll even show you how they worked out the price.
  > Let me go. Tap below.
- **Shots:** house face close-up → drip from ceiling, house winces → basement, house sniffs → tall grass → a bill flying out the mailbox → the house shrugs, paint flakes → a truck from [Your Company] pulls up → a clipboard with the offer math → the house waves goodbye → form + brand.

### Ad 3 – Skeleton story – "Still waiting for an offer"
Failed solution from the research: the listing that sits, then the price cuts.
- **Hook:** a skeleton on a porch next to a faded FOR SALE sign. On screen: HE'S STILL WAITING FOR AN OFFER
- **Script:**
  > He listed his house in the spring.
  > Twelve showings. Great feedback. Zero offers.
  > His agent said lower the price. So he did. Then he did it again.
  > And every month the mortgage, taxes and lawn bill kept coming.
  > Some houses don't sell on the open market. That's not your fault.
  > We buy houses as they are, on a date you pick.
  > Don't wait like him. Tap below.
- **Shots:** skeleton on porch, cobwebs → spring flowers, sign goes up → people walk through, thumbs up, leave → a price tag crossed out, crossed out again → calendar pages flying, bills stacking on his lap → the sign fades → a living seller shaking hands with [Your Company] → form + brand.
- Keep it light and funny. The skeleton is the joke, not the seller.

### Ad 4 – Crochet – "The roof, the furnace, the kitchen"
- **Hook:** a crochet grandma in her crochet living room, a drip of yarn falling from the ceiling into a bucket. On screen: THE HOUSE NEEDS MORE THAN YOU CAN AFFORD
- **Script:**
  > The roof needs work. So does the furnace. And the kitchen.
  > The quotes add up to more than you have.
  > And a bank won't lend a buyer money on a house that needs this much.
  > You don't have to fix any of it.
  > We buy houses in the shape yours is in.
  > We walk through it with you and show you how we got the number.
  > No pressure to say yes. Tap below to ask.
- **Shots:** yarn drip into bucket → crochet furnace puffing smoke → cracked crochet cabinets → repair quotes piling up on the table → a crochet bank with a closed sign → she sits back, relieved → a friendly crochet buyer walking through with her, notepad → the notepad math → she makes tea, calm → form + brand.
- Warm, slow, respectful. This persona fears being taken advantage of.

### Ad 5 – Singing animation – "The tenant song"
- **Hook:** a cartoon landlord at a mailbox, empty, starts to sing. On screen: MY TENANT HASN'T PAID IN MONTHS
- **Lyrics (short, catchy, 4 lines + chorus):**
  > My tenant hasn't paid since March,
  > the lawyer's bill just broke my heart,
  > the judge said "come back in the fall",
  > I don't want this house at all.
  > *(Chorus)* Sell it with the tenant in, let somebody else begin, [Your Company], tap below.
- **Shots:** empty mailbox → lawyer handing him a bill → courtroom calendar flipping → him with his head on the desk → chorus: a [Your Company] person shakes his hand, the tenant stays inside, landlord walks off lighter → form + brand.
- Only run this if you really buy houses with tenants in them. Make the joke about the process, not the tenant.

### Ad 6 – Whiteboard explainer – "How a cash offer is worked out"
Answers the #1 objection ("you'll lowball me") with the math ref 2 left in its caption.
- **Hook:** a hand draws a house and writes a big "?" next to it. On screen: WHY CASH OFFERS LOOK LOW
- **Script:**
  > Why do cash offers look low? Here's the real math.
  > Start with what your house would sell for fixed up.
  > Take out the repairs it needs.
  > Take out our costs to hold it and sell it, and our profit.
  > That's the offer.
  > Now the other way: the repairs come out of your pocket, plus agent fees, closing costs, and months of payments while it sits.
  > Sometimes listing wins. Sometimes cash wins. We'll show you both numbers. Tap below.
- **Shots:** hand-drawn, one line = one drawing. Two columns at the end: LIST IT vs SELL FOR CASH, each with its own subtractions. Show no dollar amounts unless they are your real example.
- "Sometimes listing wins" is the line that makes it trusted. Keep it.

### Ad 7 – UGC founder talking head – "Yes, we're one of those"
Fixes ref 3: same contrarian energy, but with proof in place of a claim about competitors.
- **Hook:** founder, selfie camera, in front of a house they bought. On screen: YES, WE'RE ONE OF THOSE CASH BUYERS
- **Script:**
  > Yes, we're one of those "we buy houses" companies. You're right to be careful.
  > So here's how to check any of us.
  > One: ask for proof of funds in the company's name.
  > Two: ask if they'll assign your contract to someone else. We don't. [only if true]
  > Three: ask how they got the number. We'll show you ours on paper.
  > If a buyer won't do those three things, walk away. Even if it's us.
  > Tap below.
- **Shots:** founder talking, cut away on each point: a proof-of-funds letter (real, blurred account numbers) → a contract, the word "assigns" circled → the offer worksheet → back to founder → form + brand.
- Use your real founder. If you make an AI version with Seedance, it must not claim to be a real person it isn't.

### Ad 8 – 3D explainer (Zack D Films style) – "You pick the move date"
- **Hook:** tiny 3D family standing on a giant calendar. On screen: WHAT IF YOU PICKED THE MOVE DATE?
- **Script:**
  > New job. New city. Or just a fresh start. And the house has to go.
  > Listing means showings with three kids at home, and a buyer's loan that can fall through.
  > Here's what selling for cash looks like.
  > Day one: you tell us about the house.
  > Next: we walk through it and show you our offer and how we got it.
  > Then you pick the closing date. [X] days or [Y] months. Your call.
  > Tap below to see your number.
- **Shots:** 3D mini family on a calendar → a moving truck → a messy house during a showing, kids hiding toys → a loan paper tearing in half → the "day one" step: a phone call → walk-through → a giant pen circling a date → family driving off → form + brand.

## Part 4 – Test plan

1. Launch all 8 with the same form, budget and audience, so the only difference is the ad.
2. Judge the hook by 3-second hold rate, the ad by form fills per dollar.
3. Take the winning ad and cut 3 new openings for it (the same trick refs 3 and 5 used).
4. Keep persona, format and hook in the ad name, for example `A1_clay_heir_label`, so results show which angle won.
