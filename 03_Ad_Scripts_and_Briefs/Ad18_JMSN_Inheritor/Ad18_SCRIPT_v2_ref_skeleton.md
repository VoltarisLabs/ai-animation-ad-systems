## 1. Why v1 had no real hook

v1 opened with "Don't clean out your parents' house just yet." over a dusty storeroom.
- **No money, no reward.** It named no stake: no mortgage, no cash, no offer. It was curiosity with nothing promised, which is the weak kind (`11_Psychological_Hooks/README.md` rule 4: "Curiosity only works with a specific benefit attached").
- **A still, grey first picture.** It showed no reward, no motion and no house.
- **It skipped three beats that every reference uses:** the objection sweep, the tiny ask, and the money in the deal (§2 beats 1, 4, 6).

v1 passed the process checks on paper ("buy" at 3.07 s). But the checks tested *when* the offer is heard, not *whether the hook carries a stake*. That gap is reported to the user; the locked process file wasn't edited.

## 2. How the reference scripts hook people: the 8-beat skeleton

All three talking scripts (R1, R2, R3) run the same beats in nearly the same order. R4 is a shorter cut of beats 1, 3, 5 and 7. Times are measured from the reels.

| # | Beat | R1 (216 days) | R2 (12 live copies) | R3 | The psychological lever |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook: a money stake + "you" + the house, in one breath** | 0.00 "Don't sell your house without getting bonus cash." | 0.00 "Why are you still paying the mortgage for a house you want to get rid of?" | 0.00 "Send me your address and I'll show you what I can offer for your house..." | Money is at stake in the first sentence: cash to gain, or a mortgage to lose. It uses a negative word ("Don't", "still"). The call-out names a private feeling ("a house you want to get rid of"). These are Meta's value-promise, question and statement-of-intent hooks. |
| 2 | **The turn: a person takes charge** | 2.70 "I can buy your house fast..." | 3.20 "Here's what I'll do." | 5.18 "I want to buy your house." | The gap the hook opened starts closing within about 0.4 to 3 s. People drop a curiosity gap when the answer is far away (Loewenstein). A first person ("I") makes the promise, not a company. |
| 3 | **The deal in fragments** | "Any condition, any situation, any price range." | "I'll buy your house... 100% cash. No closing costs or fees. ... That's the deal." (closed at 12.66 s) | "Today. Cash. No conditions. No games. No fine print." | One idea per beat. Each fragment gets its own caption and picture. Short, easy words feel truer (fluency). "That's the deal" closes the frame like a handshake. |
| 4 | **The sweep: the worst case, then "I'll still buy it"** | (condition line above) | "Whether the home is in foreclosure, has tax liens, or needs major work. Doesn't matter. I'll still buy it." | "Doesn't matter if ... hasn't had a repair done in 20 years. I'll buy it anyway." | It names what the seller is embarrassed about, out loud, then accepts it. Shame and objections go in one line. |
| 5 | **Effort removal by repetition** | "You don't have to clean, repair, or even empty the place." | "I don't need inspections. I don't need repairs and I don't need it cleaned." | "No inspection. No cleaning. No renovation." | Each repeat removes one more reason to wait. The action gets easier (Fogg: ability). |
| 6 | **The tiny ask** | "All you'll need to do is answer a few simple questions." | "I just need your address." | "You don't have to do a single thing to that house, except tell me where it is." | It makes step one feel like nothing. |
| 7 | **The CTA with its steps, early** | 20.46 s (48.1%) "Just tap the button right below this video..." | 32.00 s (62.8%) "Click the link below. Fill out a quick short form..." + "Simple as that." | 17.14 s (31.5%) | They ask before half the viewers have left, and say exactly what happens next. |
| 8 | **The close + CTA 2** | "Don't wait on this. Hit the button below." | Scarcity: "We cap how many homes we buy each month..." | Scarcity: "once these slots are gone..." | Their pressure is a fake limit, which we can't use. **Ours:** close the hook's loop, reverse the risk ("Don't like my number? You walk away."), then ask again. |

**The pictures do the same job:**
- **Frame 1 is one of three things:**
  - the reward: R1's bed of cash;
  - a satisfying process: R2's and R3's DIY hands;
  - the house in motion: R4's drive-by.
- **There is one picture per phrase.**
- **Faces react on the "you" payoff lines.** Emotions pass from face to face (emotional contagion), and joy and surprise cut skipping (Ref_Ads_Research §4).

**What we can't copy**, and what each script uses instead (confirmed terms only):

| They say | Why we can't | We say |
|---|---|---|
| "bonus cash" | There is no bonus | "seeing my cash offer" (a free look at the number) |
| "this week", "24 hours", "7 days" | Speed isn't confirmed | "You pick the closing date." |
| "foreclosure, tax liens" | Not confirmed that we buy those | Condition only: roof, yard, years untouched (as-is) |
| "nothing taken out" | A mortgage payoff still comes out at closing | "No commission. No fees. No closing costs." |
| "No inspections" | Not confirmed; only as-is is | "I don't need repairs." |
| caps, slots, "now or never" | A fake limit (FTC dark patterns) | Close the loop plus "Don't like my number? You walk away." |
