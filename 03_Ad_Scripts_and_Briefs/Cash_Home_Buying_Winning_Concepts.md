## 2. What the evidence says

### Finding 1: Attacking the category wins attention (refs 3 and 5)
- **Evidence:** the most total views (2,342,271), and the longest time since upload (172 days).
- **Why it points to a winner:** the advertiser cut the same script twice with different openings. You don't usually keep testing a concept that doesn't work. Treat this as an inference, not a proven fact.
- **Why it works:**
  - The hook agrees with the viewer's own doubt: "Have you seen all these ads saying they'll buy your property above market value? That's all a lie."
  - It matches the #1 and #2 objections in the research: "It's a scam" and "You're going to lowball me".
- **Weak spot:**
  - It makes a claim about competitors it can't prove ("below half the value of your house, guaranteed").
  - "We'll buy your property at valued price" gives no proof of its own.
- **The opening matters:** same script, different first shot, and the demolition opening got 2.3 times the views of the other (2,342,271 vs 1,019,339).

### Finding 2: A pain-first question with a direct offer gets the fastest traction (ref 1)
- **Evidence:** the highest views per day, 16,999.
- **Why it works:**
  - The first line names an ongoing cost the viewer feels right now: "Why are you still paying the mortgage for a house you want to get rid of?"
  - That matches the research pain points "Stop the money leak" and "The inherited house nobody wants".
- **Weak spot:**
  - The second half is hype: "We cap how many homes we buy each month… now or never."
  - The research says this audience reads hype as a scam signal.
  - At 51 s it is the longest of the five.

### Finding 3: A fake customer and soft empathy got little reach (refs 6 and 2)
- **Ref 6** (actor speaking as a past customer): 77 views/day.
  - The concept is also a legal risk under FTC 16 CFR 465.2 and 255.1.
- **Ref 2** (claymation, empathy): 13 views/day.
  - Its tone matches the research best.
  - Its hook is vague: "Nobody told you it would feel like this."
- **The strongest idea in ref 2 is hidden.** Its post text shows the offer math: the agent path vs the cash path, with repair costs, commission and months of payments. That math never appears in the video itself.

### Finding 4: Nobody owns transparency yet
- **All five ads list the same features:** no repairs, no fees, no showings, no inspections.
- **Four of the five promise speed or cash without explaining the number.**
- **None shows how the offer is worked out on screen,** even though the research says this is the proof a skeptic wants.
- **This is the open gap in the market,** judging from these five ads.

## 3. Winning concepts for Property Abundance

Ranked by fit: evidence from the refs, times fit with the research, times how safe the claims are.

| Rank | Concept | Built from | Main persona | Objection it answers | Awareness level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The honest cash buyer.** Call out the category like refs 3 and 5, then prove you're different by showing the offer math on screen. | Ref 3/5 hook + ref 2's caption math + research Finding 4 | The skeptic (all personas) | "It's a scam", "You'll lowball me" | Solution-aware: they've seen the ads and don't trust them |
| 2 | **Still paying for a house you don't want.** Ref 1's pain-question hook, aimed at one persona per ad, with no hype. | Ref 1 hook + research pain points | Inherited Irene, Tired Landlord Larry | "Hidden fees", "A realtor would get me more" | Problem-aware: they know the pain but haven't picked a fix |
| 3 | **Cash vs listing, side by side.** Put the math from ref 2's post into the video: what each path leaves in your pocket, and admit when listing wins. | Ref 2 caption + research misconception "listing costs nothing" | Fixer-Upper Fran, Moving-On Maya | "A realtor would get me more" | Solution-aware |
| 4 | **How to spot a bad cash buyer.** A checklist: proof of funds, no assigning the contract, explained offer. | Research objections (wholesaler, proof of funds) + ref 2's red and green flags | The skeptic | "You're just a wholesaler", "Do you even have the money?" | Most-aware: already comparing buyers |

### Concepts to avoid
- Actor or AI character presented as a real past customer (ref 6). This is an FTC risk.
- Fake scarcity or urgency: "we cap how many homes", "before it's too late" (refs 1, 3, 5).
- Claims about competitors you can't prove (refs 3, 5).
- Cash fanning or money-counting shots (ref 6). They look like the scam ads the audience fears.
- Any number (days to close, hours to offer, price) that isn't Property Abundance's real, measured fact.

### What each winning concept depends on (must be true first)
| Concept | Needs from Property Abundance |
|---|---|
| 1 and 3 | A real offer worksheet you're willing to show: value − repairs − costs/margin. |
| 2 | Real close timeline and fee policy, for any stated promise. |
| 4 | You don't assign contracts, and you can show proof of funds in the company's name. |
| All | A live website or form. The domain returned HTTP 403 on 2026-09-22. |
