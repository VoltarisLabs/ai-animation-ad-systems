# Swipe File: Annotated Example Ads

These are original training examples written for this playbook across different industries, all in our two formats: **short-form video and static**. They are not real campaigns and their numbers are placeholders. Each one is annotated so writers can see *why* it works. Add real winners at the bottom.

---

### 1. B2B SaaS, problem-aware, Meta static (PAS)
> **Image text:** Still chasing late invoices?
> **Primary:** You finished the work 45 days ago. You're still sending "just checking in" emails. [Tool] sends automatic, polite reminders and a one-click pay link, so clients pay in days, not weeks. Try it free for 14 days.
> **Headline:** Get paid in days, not weeks

**Why it works:** The hook is a pain in the customer's own words. "45 days" and "just checking in" are specific, recognizable details. The mechanism is named (automatic reminders + pay link). The first step is free and risk-free.

### 2. Local service (dentist), solution-aware, Meta static (big claim)
> **Image:** A dentist showing a patient their 3D smile scan on a tablet. **Image text:** "See your new smile before you commit."
> **Primary:** Free 3D scan and consult. Same-week appointments. Payment plans from $99/mo. Book in 60 seconds.
> **Headline:** Free 3D smile scan

**Why it works:** "See it before you commit" is risk reversal: the scary part of the decision (paying for something you can't picture) is removed. The image shows the *process*, not a before/after result, which keeps it inside Meta's health and appearance rules. The price is broken into a monthly amount. The CTA sets expectations ("60 seconds").

### 3. Fitness app, unaware, 30s UGC (Star-Story-Solution), full script sheet
| Time | Visual / b-roll | Spoken line | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2s | Creator on a couch, gym bag by the door, tags still on | "Every January I buy a gym plan." | Every January... |
| 2-6s | Calendar flipping to February, bag still unopened | "By February I've quit. Every year." | ...quit by Feb |
| 6-12s | Phone screen: a 60-minute workout plan | "Turns out the plans were 60 minutes long. I have 15." | 60 min plan. 15 min life. |
| 12-20s | App building a 12-minute session around a calendar | "[App] builds 12-minute workouts around my actual schedule." | 12-minute workouts |
| 20-26s | Creator mid-workout at home, timer showing 12:00 | "I'm on week 11. First time ever." | Week 11 |
| 26-30s | End card: app screen + button | "If that sounds like your January, try the free week." | Try the free week |

**Alternate 0-2s hooks (body stays identical):**
1. Visual: gym bag with tags on. Spoken: "I've paid for 6 gym plans I never used." Text: 6 plans. 0 finished.
2. Visual: 60-minute timer. Spoken: "The problem was never motivation." Text: It wasn't motivation.
3. Visual: phone calendar full of meetings. Spoken: "I don't have an hour. I have 15 minutes." Text: I have 15 minutes.

**Why it works:** The hook is a relatable moment, not a product. It moves the blame from the viewer to the plan ("it wasn't motivation"), without labeling the viewer. The mechanism (12 minutes) answers the real cause. "Week 11" is specific proof. The CTA refers to their situation ("your January"), not a trait.

### 4. Skincare e-commerce, product-aware, retargeting Reels video (objection-buster)
> **Hook text:** "Won't this break me out?"
> **Body:** It's the #1 question we get. Our formula has 0 fragrance and 0 essential oils, and it was tested on sensitive skin. If you react, we refund the full bottle. Even if it's empty.

**Why it works:** It raises the worry before the viewer can. The proof is specific. The guarantee is bold and removes the risk. Only people who already know the product see it, so it doesn't need to explain the product.

### 5. Online course, most aware, Stories static (offer card)
> **Image text:** "Enrollment closes Thursday."
> **Primary:** Join by midnight Thursday and get the 5 client-pitch templates free. The next cohort starts Monday.

**Why it works:** Most-aware buyers need the offer only. The deadline is real and tied to a cohort start. The bonus removes the next objection ("but how do I get clients?").

### 6. Agency, product-aware, Meta static (us vs. them table)
> **Image text:** Them: reports. / Us: booked calls.
> **Primary:** Last quarter: 312 meetings for 9 B2B clients, every one logged in their CRM. We take 3 new clients a month.
> **Headline:** Calls, not reports

**Why it works:** A two-row contrast is readable in one second. The contrast is an output, not an activity. The numbers are specific. The capacity limit is true scarcity and also frames them as the prize (Klaff).

### 7. Home services, problem-aware, TikTok short-form video (listicle)
> "3 signs your water heater is about to quit: 1) rusty water, 2) rumbling sounds, 3) it's over 10 years old. If you hit 2 of 3, get a free check before it floods your basement."

**Why it works:** It's useful on its own, so it earns attention even from non-buyers. It lets viewers self-diagnose (commitment). The loss framing is accurate (flooding). The first step is free.

### 8. Real estate (cash buyer), problem-aware, Meta static (Housing category)
> **Primary:** Inherited a house you don't want to deal with? We buy as-is: no cleanouts, no repairs, no showings. You pick the closing date. Get a no-obligation cash offer.

**Why it works:** The situation call-out does the targeting, which matters because Housing-category ads can't target by demographics. It removes three kinds of effort at once. "You pick the date" gives the seller control, which lowers fear. It names the task ("a house you don't want to deal with"), not the loss behind it. Never mention death, illness or divorce directly.
