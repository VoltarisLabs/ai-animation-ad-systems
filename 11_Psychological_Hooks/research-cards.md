# Research Cards: Five Seller Avatars

**What this is.** Pre-filled Part-A research cards for all five Property Abundance seller avatars, so a script can be written one-shot with zero new research. Each card follows the Part A format in [templates/script-and-scene-breakdown.md](templates/script-and-scene-breakdown.md), extended with trigger moments, hook levers, hook killers and vocabulary. Pick the avatar, copy the card into the ad file, write Part B from it. Every line in a script must trace to a field on the card; if it doesn't, cut it.

**Sources.** Everything here traces to `04_Audience_Research/Research_Document_Cash_Home_Buying.md` (the research file: 17 Reddit Answers responses, 170 threads, collected 2026-09-22), `04_Audience_Research/Buyer_Avatar/README.md` (the avatar foundation) and `04_Audience_Research/Buyer_Avatar/Psychological_Profile_and_Hook_Map.md` (the Hook Map). Nothing below is new data.

**Tags.** [R] = in the research data (a quote, pain, objection or persona detail). [I] = inference carried over from the Hook Map. [D] = derived here for avatars 4 and 5, which the Hook Map does not profile individually, by applying its cross-avatar doctrine to the research file's persona data. Test [I] and [D] material; do not quote it as fact.

**Quote sweep (2026-09-29).** Every string presented as a quote on these cards was checked against the `04_Audience_Research` files (the research document, the raw file and the Buyer_Avatar folder). A bare [R] on a quoted string now means that exact string appears in the research document or the raw Reddit data. Lines that exist only in the Buyer_Avatar README's Representative Voice or "likely objections" sections are invented avatar language, not sourced quotes: they carry [I] or an explicit "[R paraphrase, README]" label, never a bare [R]. Two mistags were fixed in this sweep (Card 2's two-months line, Card 3's give-me-a-number line). Note: the Hook Map still tags both lines [R] (its lines ~200 and ~263); on those two lines this file is the corrected record.

**Rule 1: quotes are internal only.** Every quoted line on these cards is a Reddit commenter's words or representative avatar language. They exist to give the writer wording and tone. They never go on screen, in a voiceover, in post text or in a caption, and they are never presented as customer testimonials (FTC 16 CFR 255.1, 16 CFR 465.2). Rewrite before anything ships.

**Rule 2: claims come only from the claims registry.** No offer term, number, timeline or capability appears in an ad unless [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) lists it as confirmed. The research file marks the usual terms (24-hour offer, 7-day close, no fees, no repairs, buys occupied, direct purchase, proof of funds) as PLACEHOLDER or TBD. The "Confirmed claims" field on every card below therefore points at the registry instead of listing terms. If the registry does not confirm a claim, the script works without it.

**Rule 3: every hook passes the three gates** (Hook Map): situation gate (the right person recognises themselves in under one second), predator gate (the line could not have been written by the scammer they already fear), truth gate (no unconfirmed assertion). Universal banned language, all avatars: *top dollar*, *best price guaranteed*, *we pay more than anyone* [R: "Readers call these lies"], plus [I]: *distressed*, *desperate*, *motivated seller*, *act now*, *limited time*, *don't miss out*, *hassle-free*, *we care about you*, any countdown, any stock-photo handshake energy.

Scene staging notes (rooms, objects, light) are writer's craft layered on [R] facts; the facts underneath each scene are cited.

## Card 1: Emily the Heir (Overwhelmed Inheritor)

**Audience + moment:** Adult child who inherited a parent's house hours away, in the months when grief and estate paperwork overlap: taxes, insurance and upkeep are running on a house she does not want, and the family cannot agree what to do with it. [R]

**Awareness level:** Problem-aware, solution-skeptical. She knows cash buyers exist and already believes they pay below market; the hook's job is disarming, not education. [I]

**Bullseye desire (her words):** "I just wanted it SOLD." / "just want to be done with it." The full shape of the deal she would take: "I'd consider a cash offer, as is, where we set a date, the condition of the home (empty, tenants, whatever) and I move. Cut out all the bullshit." [R]

**Top pains (their words):**
- "I live six hours away from the house I inherited and I'm getting tired of driving back for stupid stuff." [R]
- "I'm worried about being sad and grieving at the same time I will have to deal with all of their logistics and bills and money and inheritance. It's crazy it all hits at once." [R]
- "My sibling doesn't want to put any money into it but refuses to consider a cash offer." [R]

**Top fear / objection (exact words):** "Am I getting ripped off because I just want this house gone?" [R paraphrase, README]. Underneath it: "Is my exhaustion being priced in against me?" [I]. The lowball evidence she has already read: "they offered me $78k when I had multiple realtors tell me it's worth somewhere around $190k." [R] Deepest fear: being blamed by the family for letting the family home go too cheap. [I; the family-conflict pain is R]

**What she already tried and why it fails:**
- Listing with a realtor at a high price: the agent prices high to win the listing, then cuts it over and over ("She was very sure at $850k but now says $710 is the max"). [R]
- Renting it out: "managing tenants from this far away sounds like it could just turn into another thing on my plate lol." [R]
- Fixing it up first: no money, time or energy to manage contractors; big renovations rarely pay back. [R]
- Doing nothing: "A vacant house is a problem that can lead to bigger problems." [R]

**Unspoken sentence:** "I want this house gone, and I feel guilty for wanting that." She is buying permission to be done: absolution, not persuasion. [I]

**Scenes (dimensionalized):**
1. Her own kitchen counter, six hours from the property: the forwarded property tax bill for a house she does not live in, sitting on top of her regular mail. [R: tax and insurance bills on an unwanted house; distance]
2. The parent's bedroom closet: clothes still on the hangers, flat-packed boxes leaning unopened against the wall. [Hook Map trigger: the closet she cannot face; R: cleaning out a parent's belongings]
3. The interstate at dusk, hour five of the drive back, gas receipts in the cupholder, for one broken gutter. [R: "driving back for stupid stuff"]
4. The family group text: her photo of the repair estimate, the sibling's one-word "no" underneath it. [R: sibling refuses repairs and refuses the cash offer]
5. The vacant house at night from the street: every window dark, one lamp on a timer, lit porches on both sides. [R: "A vacant house is a problem that can lead to bigger problems"]
6. The listing sheet on her fridge with the asking price crossed out twice in the realtor's pen. [R: repeated price cuts]

**Trigger moments (when she is reachable):** the property tax or insurance bill lands; she gets back from another round trip "for stupid stuff"; she opens the closet with the parent's clothes still in it; a sibling says no for the third time; the realtor drops the price again. [R/I, Hook Map]

**Hook levers (Hook Map):** 1. Absolution: make stopping the responsible choice. 2. Task subtraction: every hook removes something (the drive, the clean-out, the sorting). 3. The unspoken sentence, said out loud. 4. Family-blame shield: give her the line she can hand to her siblings. 5. Grief-adjacent, never grief-exploiting: acknowledge, do not perform sympathy. 6. Distance: "you live hours away" is instant self-identification.

**Hook killers (Hook Map):** hype urgency ("act now", "limited"): she reads it as predation. Sympathy theatre ("we know how hard it is to lose a parent"): feels like a script. Price-led openers: confirm the lowball fear before any trust exists. Any multi-step process in the hook: she is buying fewer tasks.

**Her vocabulary (internal, rewrite before use):** as-is, clean-out, probate, title, the estate, comps, Zestimate, hoarder house, "driving back for stupid stuff", "another thing on my plate", "just want to be done with it". [R]

**Confirmed claims we may use:** only what [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) confirms at time of writing. Nothing on this card is a claim.

**Banned in this ad:** the universal banned list (header, Rule 3); any unconfirmed offer term (speed, fees, repairs, direct purchase); any Reddit quote on screen; sympathy theatre; urgency; price-led opening.

## Card 2: Marcus Behind on Payments (Financially Cornered Homeowner)

**Audience + moment:** Homeowner with remaining equity who has missed mortgage payments or property taxes after a job loss or life event, researching his options alone at night so nobody finds out, while lender letters accumulate. [R]

**Awareness level:** Problem-aware, urgently; solution-aware but threat-flagged. He does not need the problem explained. He needs a route that does not feel like a trap, told calmly. [I]

**Bullseye desire:** "a clear timeline and a straight answer" [R, persona interests: the research file's persona wording, not a Reddit quote]. In the README's Representative Voice, which is invented avatar language, [I], not sourced quotes: "I need somebody to just tell me what my options are." / "How much time do I actually have?" Underneath: stop the foreclosure, protect the credit, keep whatever equity is left. [R]

**Top pains (their words):**
- "Can my house get forclosed after being 3 months behind?" (post title, spelling as posted) [R]
- "The longer you wait the less options you have." [R]
- "I can probably manage for another two months at most." [I for this persona. The line is a real Golden Nuggets quote, but it is a non-US landlord's words (r/AusPropertyChat, kept in the research file for tone only), not a cornered US homeowner's. Treat it as inference here: tone reference only, never presented as his words, never on screen, never a claim.]

**Top fear / objection (exact words):** "You're trying to take advantage of me because you know I'm in trouble" [R paraphrase, README]; he has read that buyers "focus on the three Ds: death, divorce, destitution." [R] His distrust is technical, not vague: "Proof of funds letters are often drawn on some vague LLC that they don't even own." [R] And the restart he cannot survive: "2 months later they offered 10% less than originally quoted so by that time I didn't want to start over with a realtor." [R] Deepest fear: his family finding out how bad it got, and losing the equity and the credit both. [I; the equity/credit fear is R]

**What he already tried and why it fails:**
- Calling his servicer and searching options late at night: information without a decision. [R]
- Waiting, because delay feels like hope: every month closes options ("The longer you wait the less options you have"). [R/I]
- Considering cash buyers: flagged as predators who hunt people like him, with fake proof of funds. [R]

**Unspoken sentence:** "I don't want anyone to know how bad it's gotten." He wants a competent, non-judgmental person to lay the options on the table so the decision makes itself. [I]

**Scenes (dimensionalized):**
1. The front hallway: a certified-mail slip from the servicer, still not taken to the post office. [R: letters from the lender]
2. 2 a.m., a dark bedroom, phone glow: the search bar holding half of "can my house get foreclosed after being 3 months behind." [R: searching his options late at night]
3. The kitchen table after everyone is asleep: banking app open, counting how many mortgage payments the savings still cover. [I staging, toned on the non-US two-months quote above; the R basis is Persona 2: money is tight, and "The longer you wait the less options you have"]
4. The mail pile: lender envelopes set apart, unopened, from the rest of the letters. [R: letters, fear, shame]
5. A notice with a date on it, held in his hand, the date read twice. [Hook Map trigger]

**Trigger moments (when he is reachable):** a certified letter from the servicer arrives; the 2 a.m. search; the moment he calculates how many months of savings are left; a notice with a date on it. [R/I, Hook Map]

**Hook levers (Hook Map):** 1. Calm: a lower emotional temperature than the situation; calm reads as competence. 2. Time stated as fact, never as pressure ("here's what happens at day 120", not "act now"). 3. Self-incrimination: admit the downside of your own offer first; volunteering the negative is the fastest credibility purchase available. 4. Option-mapping, not selling: include options that are not you, such as a HUD-approved housing counselor. 5. Privacy: imply discretion. 6. Non-judgment: never "distressed", never "desperate".

**Hook killers (Hook Map):** countdowns, "before it's too late", manufactured scarcity: the research explicitly warns against urgency hype that preys on distress. The words *distressed*, *desperate*, *struggling*, *facing foreclosure* aimed at him: labels the shame. Big promises with no mechanism: he is trained to spot the vague LLC. Overly warm, high-energy delivery: reads as a closer.

**His vocabulary (internal, rewrite before use):** behind on payments, underwater, equity, earnest money, proof of funds, "and assigns", servicer, comps, lowball, credit, options. [R]

**Confirmed claims we may use:** only what [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) confirms at time of writing. Nothing on this card is a claim.

**Banned in this ad:** the universal banned list; any deadline or countdown; the shame labels above; any unconfirmed offer term; any Reddit quote on screen; specific day-counts or foreclosure-timeline figures unless the registry confirms them.

## Card 3: Jason the Burned-Out Landlord

**Audience + moment:** Side investor with a day job whose rental has a non-paying tenant; the eviction keeps getting continued, attorney invoices keep arriving, and he is covering the property's mortgage out of his own savings. [R]

**Awareness level:** Solution-aware, most-aware on a good day. He has already considered cash buyers and has probably been burned by or warned about a wholesaler. The hook's job is qualification: prove the tenant is not a deal breaker, or he is gone in one second. [I/R]

**Bullseye desire:** "Just give me a number and let me decide." [I: inferred, not a sourced quote. It appears nowhere in the Reddit data, only in the README's Representative Voice (invented avatar language) and in the Hook Map, whose [R] tag on it was wrong, as was the one here. His sourced words are in Top pains below: "Bleeding money for months on an attorney is gutting me." and "I honestly feel like I'm a victim and the tenant is a bully here."] Stated: sell it, stop the bleed, stop dealing with tenants. Hidden: a clean transfer of the problem is worth more than a higher number; the loss stops being his the day he signs. [R/I]

**Top pains (their words):**
- "Bleeding money for months on an attorney is gutting me." [R]
- "I honestly feel like I'm a victim and the tenant is a bully here." [R]
- No rent coming in, legal fees, damage to the property. [R, persona pain points]

**Top fear / objection (exact words):** "You're not actually going to buy this property with the tenant still there." [R paraphrase, README] The wholesaler trap he has been warned about: "Many 'cash buyers' now are just wholesalers who tie you up in contract and then try to sell the deal to me and skim the middle." [R] Deepest fear: that he made a permanent financial mistake and there is no clean exit, only a slower bleed. [I]

**What he already tried and why it fails:**
- Eviction through the courts: slow, hearings continued, some judges do not grant it. [R]
- Cash for keys: paying the tenant to leave costs money too. [R]
- Keeping the attorney on it: "Bleeding money for months on an attorney is gutting me." [R]
- Waiting for the tenant situation to resolve so he can list: the mortgage is still due monthly out of his own pocket. [R]

**Unspoken sentence:** "Tell me I'm not stupid, and that the loss stops being mine the day I sign." [I]

**Scenes (dimensionalized):**
1. The courthouse hallway bench: the attorney leaning over to say the hearing got continued again; a new date scribbled on the folder. [R: evictions drag on for months]
2. The day's mail: the attorney's itemized invoice sitting on top of the rental's mortgage statement. [R: attorney fees stack up while the mortgage runs]
3. Rent day, phone in hand: banking app refreshed twice, the deposit line still empty. [R: tenants stop paying]
4. The curb outside the rental: new damage visible from the driveway; he does not go in. [R: damage to the property]
5. His day-job desk: taking a call about the property between meetings, savings covering that month's mortgage. [R: side investor with a day job, using savings to cover the mortgage]

**Trigger moments (when he is reachable):** the eviction hearing gets continued again; the attorney invoice arrives; rent day passes with nothing; he walks the property and sees new damage. [R, Hook Map]

**Hook levers (Hook Map):** 1. Qualification in the first second: the word *occupied* or *tenant still in it* is the hook (claim-dependent: only if the registry confirms buying occupied). 2. Arithmetic of the bleed: he thinks in monthly loss; speak in it. 3. Finality: the promise is not money, it is "it stops being yours." 4. Anti-wholesaler positioning: the one credential he cares about (claim-dependent). 5. No process language: "quick form", "we'll walk you through it" kills it. 6. Permission to stop: cutting losses reframed as the competent move, not surrender.

**Hook killers (Hook Map):** anything that sounds like a form, a process or a walkthrough. Sympathy: he wants competence, not comfort. Hedging on the tenant question: one second of ambiguity and he is gone. Talking about the house's potential: he has heard it.

**His vocabulary (internal, rewrite before use):** eviction, cash for keys, bleeding money, attorney fees, non-paying tenant, occupied, turn, month-to-month, court date, cut my losses. [R]

**Confirmed claims we may use:** only what [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) confirms at time of writing. The occupied-purchase and no-assignment angles are unusable until the registry confirms them. Nothing on this card is a claim.

**Banned in this ad:** the universal banned list; process language; sympathy; any tenant-purchase or anti-wholesaler assertion the registry has not confirmed; specific dollar or month figures for his losses unless verified; any Reddit quote on screen.

## Card 4: Fran, the House That Needs Everything (Fixer-Upper / Older Homeowner)

*The Hook Map profiles avatars 1-3 only. Levers, killers and triggers below are its cross-avatar doctrine applied to the research file's Persona 4 data, tagged [D].*

**Audience + moment:** Older or retired homeowner on a fixed low income whose house needs a roof, HVAC and cosmetic work she cannot pay for, at the moment she learns that fixing it first is not an option and that ordinary buyers cannot finance it as it stands. [R: "I'm 70", "retired and on a fixed (low) income", lenders won't finance homes in bad shape]

**Awareness level:** Solution-aware and braced for insult. She has asked realtors, gathered repair quotes, and may have already filled out a cash-buyer form and been offended by the result. [D, from her activities and the "pennies" quote below]

**Bullseye desire:** "an honest price for the house as it stands." [R, persona interests: the research file's persona wording, not a Reddit quote]. How she actually phrases the problem (her words): "No Money.. How can I repair My Home before Selling." [R, spelling as posted]

**Top pains (their words):**
- "I'm 70 and hate having home repairs done." [R]
- "No Money.. How can I repair My Home before Selling" [R]
- Can't afford repairs, and lenders won't finance buyers for her house. [R, persona pain point]

**Top fear / objection (exact words):** "You'll offer me pennies because it's ugly." [R paraphrase, research persona] The experience behind it, from another seller: "I filled out their form online, they sent someone out, and then offered me pennies." [R] She is afraid of being taken advantage of, and afraid of repair bills; her values are dignity, independence, security. [R]

**What she already tried and why it fails:**
- Getting repair quotes: the numbers are beyond a fixed income with little savings and no credit cards. [R]
- Asking realtors for advice: the open-market route needs the house fixed first. [R]
- Fixing it up first: no money, and "There's almost no fix or improvement has a 100% return." [R]
- Listing as-is on the open market: buyers still inspect, bring a repair list and renegotiate, and lenders won't finance homes with roof, HVAC or foundation problems. [R]

**Unspoken sentence:** "The house got away from me, and I can't afford to catch it up." She will not say the disrepair embarrasses her; dignity is the value at stake. [D, from her values and pains]

**Scenes (dimensionalized):**
1. The kitchen table: three contractor quotes fanned out, every one larger than the savings account. [R: getting repair quotes; fixed income, little savings]
2. The hallway ceiling: the brown water stain that has grown since last winter. [R: the house needs a roof]
3. The furnace closet: the HVAC unit decades old, the repair estimate taped to the door. [R: the house needs HVAC work]
4. The front porch with the realtor: the fix-before-listing list getting longer at every room. [R: asking realtors; open market needs repairs]
5. Her own kitchen table again: the visitor a web form summoned, sliding over an offer of pennies. [R: "filled out their form online... offered me pennies"]
6. The bank statement on the first of the month: the fixed income in, the fixed bills out, nothing left over for a roof. [R: retired on a fixed low income]

**Trigger moments (when she is reachable):** a new repair quote arrives she cannot pay; another system fails (roof leak, furnace); a realtor hands her the fix-first list; a sale or inquiry dies because a buyer's lender will not finance the house. [D, each anchored in the R items above]

**Hook levers ([D], cross-avatar doctrine applied):** 1. Self-incrimination, the Hook Map's one move that outperforms on all avatars: say the bad part first ("a cash offer is lower than a fixed-up retail sale") and then show what she actually walks away with; the research names the fair, explained price as the desired outcome. 2. Dignity: the house's condition stated as a fact of old houses, never as her failure. 3. Task subtraction: no repairs, no clean-out, nothing to fix first (claim-dependent: registry must confirm). 4. Insider specificity: name the dead end only sellers like her know, that lenders will not finance a house needing roof or HVAC work, so "just list it" was never real advice. 5. The as-is misconception, corrected: listing as-is still means inspections and renegotiation; buying as-is does not. [R basis for each]

**Hook killers ([D], from the objection set and universal list):** the universal banned language. Any echo of "ugly house" marketing: it labels the shame the way "distressed" labels Marcus's. Price-led openers: she is pre-loaded with the "pennies" story. Pressure or urgency: she moves slowly and values independence; rushing reads as the con. Cheerful renovation talk or "potential": she has the quotes; she knows what potential costs.

**Her vocabulary (internal, rewrite before use):** as-is, repairs, roof, HVAC, quotes, estimates, fixed income, retired, lowball, inspection list, flipper, "no money to fix it". [R, research ICP vocabulary plus persona 4]

**Confirmed claims we may use:** only what [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) confirms at time of writing. Nothing on this card is a claim.

**Banned in this ad:** the universal banned list; "ugly", "eyesore" or any condition-shaming; unconfirmed repair/fee/speed terms; any Reddit quote on screen; renovation-fantasy imagery of her house.

## Card 5: Maya, Needs Out Fast (Life-Transition Seller)

*The Hook Map profiles avatars 1-3 only. Levers, killers and triggers below are its cross-avatar doctrine applied to the research file's Persona 5 data, tagged [D].*

**Audience + moment:** A homeowner mid life-change (divorce or a bad relationship, a new job, relocation, a growing family) who has to sell and move on a real calendar, with no time or money for fixes and showings, often with young kids in tow. [R]

**Awareness level:** Solution-aware, comparison-shopping certainty against price. She knows the cash route exists and has likely heard both the relief stories and the lowball stories; she values a sure thing over a slightly higher maybe. [R/D]

**Bullseye desire (her words):** "we set a date... and I move." [R] The relief story she wants to be telling afterwards: "Three young kids, no money for improvements/fixes, and we needed to move fast. It was the easiest process." [R]

**Top pains (their words):**
- "I needed out from a bad relationship." [R]
- No time or money for fixes and showings. [R, persona pain point]
- "I just lowered the price…. again. Hopefully the showing tomorrow will generate an offer." [R]

**Top fear / objection (exact words):** "Is the convenience worth the lower price?" [R paraphrase, research persona] The fee ambush she has read about: "open door offered me $20k less than zillows 'estimate' and then they wanted 5% service fee and 2% closing fees." [R] And the counter-story in her head: "My 'as is' house sold in 3 days for asking price using a realtor friend of mine." [R] What she cannot afford: a deal that falls through and a restart ("I did not have to worry about the financing falling through" is the outcome she wants). [R]

**What she already tried and why it fails:**
- Listing and waiting through showings: many showings, good feedback, zero offers, then another price cut, while the move date does not move. [R]
- Keeping the house show-ready with kids while working: no time, no money for fixes. [R]
- Considering the open market's "higher price": it comes with financing risk, appraisal problems and late renegotiation, the uncertainties she is selling to escape. [R: desired outcome "a closing they can count on"]

**Unspoken sentence:** "I can't hold this life together through another ninety days of showings." Certainty is the product; the date is the price she is really negotiating. [D, from her persona: busy, stretched thin, values a sure thing over a slightly higher maybe]

**Scenes (dimensionalized):**
1. The minivan in a parking lot, three kids in car seats, waiting out a showing that ends with no offer. [R: three young kids; showings with no offers]
2. The fridge calendar: the new job's start date circled, in a city hours away, weeks out. [R: new job, relocation]
3. Midnight kitchen: wiping the counters again for tomorrow's showing, moving boxes already stacked in the hallway. [R: showings; the move already underway]
4. The listing app: the "price reduced" banner on her own house. [R: "I just lowered the price…. again."]
5. The kitchen table with two calendars side by side: the move-out date she needs and the closing date nobody will promise her. [R: desired outcomes "they pick the move date", "a closing they can count on"]

**Trigger moments (when she is reachable):** another price drop goes live; a showing is scheduled into an already impossible week; the life-event date (job start, separation, lease on the next place) gets closer while the house sits; a buyer's financing wobbles or a deal falls through. [D, each anchored in the R items above]

**Hook levers ([D], cross-avatar doctrine applied):** 1. Certainty over maybe: sell the closing that cannot fall through in place of the higher offer that might (claim-dependent: registry must confirm the certainty terms). 2. The date she picks: her move date as the fixed point the sale bends around (claim-dependent). 3. Task subtraction: no showings, no cleaning, no keeping it spotless with kids (claim-dependent). 4. Self-incrimination: name the honest trade first, that a cash offer is lower than a full retail sale, then compare what she actually walks away with after commission, repairs and concessions; the research's net-proceeds framing. 5. Fee transparency: name the hidden-fee ambush before she does; she has read the 5% plus 2% story. [R basis for each]

**Hook killers ([D], from the objection set and universal list):** the universal banned language. Naming her divorce or crisis at her: the research says predators "focus on the three Ds: death, divorce, destitution", so leading with her situation reads as the predator. Vague timelines ("fast", "quickly") with no confirmed mechanism: certainty is the whole purchase, and an unconfirmed speed claim also fails the truth gate. Adding steps or paperwork to the hook. Guilt about the price: she has already decided convenience can be worth it; arguing price drags her back into the doubt.

**Her vocabulary (internal, rewrite before use):** showings, open houses, as-is, closing date, move date, price drop, fell through, appraisal, financing, service fee, closing costs, "needed to move fast", "easiest process". [R, research ICP vocabulary plus persona 5]

**Confirmed claims we may use:** only what [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) confirms at time of writing. Nothing on this card is a claim.

**Banned in this ad:** the universal banned list; any mention of divorce, death or destitution aimed at the viewer; unconfirmed speed or certainty terms; any Reddit quote on screen; fee claims the registry has not confirmed.

## Using a card (one-shot script flow)

1. Pick the avatar and copy their card into the ad file as Part A.
2. Check [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md) and write the confirmed-claims line for this specific ad. If the registry confirms nothing the script needs, write the claim-free version.
3. Write Part B from the card: hook from a trigger moment or the unspoken sentence, payload from the scenes, objection beat from the top fear in their words (rewritten), honest-answer beat via self-incrimination, CTA as the smallest honest next step.
4. Score the hook on the Hook Map rubric (self-recognition, emotional precision, trust posture, specificity, truth; under 7 of 10, rewrite) and run Part D checks in [templates/script-and-scene-breakdown.md](templates/script-and-scene-breakdown.md).

**Source of record:** `04_Audience_Research/Research_Document_Cash_Home_Buying.md`. If a card and the research file ever disagree, the research file wins.
