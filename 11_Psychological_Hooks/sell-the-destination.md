# Sell the destination, not the plane (the 90/10 rule)

Applies to every script in this repo, every format: skeleton, crochet, singing, claymation, talking objects, UGC, game-look (`/gta-style`), statics. The claims registry still wins over everything here: this rule decides what the script spends its time on, the registry decides which words are allowed.

## The rule

Brian Tracy: people don't care what the product is, who you are or how your company works. They care what it does for them. About 15% of buying is driven by the past and 85% by the future they picture after buying. They buy a change in their life: an improvement, a result, a benefit or, highest of all, a transformation.

If you sell a trip to the Canary Islands, spend 90% of the time on the islands (palm trees, warm water) and 10% on the plane. Most ads do the opposite: they show the plane diagram, the seats and the food.

- **The plane** is anything about how it works: the process, the offer terms, the company, the features, the steps.
- **The destination** is the customer's life after: what they stop dealing with, what they get back, what they get to do next.

## How it shows up in a script

1. **Budget for a cold ad.** About 40% pain (it earns the scroll), about 15% the turn (the mechanism, in one line), about 45% destination. The mechanism never runs longer than one line.
2. **Every feature becomes a moment in their life.** If a line could sit on a pricing page, it's the plane. Keep the approved words, and put the destination in the picture:

   | Plane (the approved line on its own) | Destination (same line, shown as life after) |
   |---|---|
   | "No repairs." | She locks the front door one last time and walks away from the tarp roof without looking back |
   | "Take what matters. Leave the rest." | She carries out one framed photo; the boxes stay behind and she doesn't care |
   | "No commission. No closing costs." | Kitchen table, no stack of bills, coffee, calm |
   | "You pick the closing date." | A calendar circled the week the grandkids visit, at the new place |

3. **The last content beat shows the next chapter, not just the problem ending.** "MISSION COMPLETE" says the pain stopped. "NEW AREA UNLOCKED" (a new place on the map, the homeowner on a porch with family) shows what they bought. End on something the person gets to do next.
4. **The Ask hands the destination to the viewer.** Visually put the viewer in the hero's place (their own empty house marker, the same win landing on it). The spoken Ask stays the registry wording: "Ask for a cash offer. No obligation."
5. **Show it, don't list it.** Destination beats need a person, a place and an action on screen, not ticks or icons.

## Check before any script ships

Tag every spoken line PAIN, PLANE or DESTINATION, and every "What we see" cell too.

- [ ] PLANE is no more than about 15% of the spoken words.
- [ ] DESTINATION is at least about 40% of the script (spoken or shown).
- [ ] The final image before the end card is someone's improved life, not the absence of the problem.
- [ ] Every feature line has a destination visual next to it.
- [ ] Every line still passes [claims-registry.md](claims-registry.md). Destination pictures carry no new claims: no money amounts, no speed, no "we buy".

## Worked example (game-look, rates angle, Oct 2026)

First draft: the back half was all plane ("a buyer who doesn't need a loan", the fee list) and about 3 seconds of life after (a sunset). Rewrite: the turn shrank to one line ("The house was never the problem. The loan was."), and every Close got a destination picture: an empty mailbox and the door locked one last time, a calm kitchen table with no bills, then the map pulls back and a new area lights up with the homeowner on a porch with grandkids. The spoken closes stayed in registry wording.
