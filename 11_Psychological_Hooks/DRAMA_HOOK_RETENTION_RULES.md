# Drama, hook and retention rules for story ads

Written 2026-10-06 from 3 research passes:
- platform data and peer-reviewed studies;
- 6 famous dramatic ads, measured from their frames;
- micro-drama industry reports.

These rules work alongside `claims-registry.md`, `sell-the-destination.md` and the nested-loop rule; they don't replace them. Applied first in the Maya storm script (2026-10-06).

## Start (0-3 s)
- **Open in the middle of the conflict or the action** (Google ABCDs; following them gave +30% short-term sales likelihood, Kantar-validated). Micro-dramas: "Drop us into the middle of conflict or action."
- **The first 3 s carry the most value:** up to 47% of a Facebook video campaign's value comes in the first 3 s, and up to 74% in the first 10 s (Facebook/Nielsen 2016).
- **Show the character's face in the first 2 s** (+50% hooking power, TikTok/CreatorIQ). Hook in picture and sound together (1.5x more likely to be a top-20% ad for purchase intent, Meta/Toluna).
- **Surprise captures attention; joy holds it** (Teixeira, Wedel & Pieters 2012).
- **Put the key message on screen within 3 s:** 63% of the highest-CTR TikTok ads did (TikTok). A narrator plus music: 2.0x for brand interest (Meta).

## Middle
- **One specific question, half-answered,** so curiosity peaks (Loewenstein 1994; Kang et al. 2009).
- **Escalate:** each new twist is bigger than the last, and the full answer is held for the end (Ely, Frankel & Kamenica 2015).
- **Re-hook at about 22% and 44% of the runtime** (the MrBeast production memo); for 45 s, that's about 10 s and 20 s. Recap the premise mid-way (Jenny Hoyos).
- **New shot every 2-4 s; new story beat every 8-10 s.** A variety of scenes gave +38% conversion (TikTok).
- **Make it easy to get lost in the story:** a picturable plot, a believable world, a character people identify with (van Laer et al. 2014). Stories shift beliefs even when labelled fiction (Green & Brock 2000).
- **Lowest point at 45-55%, then the turn at 43-68%.** That's where the turn landed in the 6 famous ads measured (Unsung Hero, Reunion, The Long Wait, Misunderstood, Lost Dog, Thank You Mom).

## Peak and reveal
- **Peak late:** people judge an ad by its peak and its end, and prefer late peaks and high endings (Baumgartner, Sujan & Padgett 1997). 4 of the 6 famous ads peak at 81-95%.
- **Bring the offer in as the plot device at the turn, before the peak.** Entertainment that comes after the brand appears lifts purchase intent (Teixeira, Picard & el Kaliouby 2014). 3 of the 6 famous ads make the brand the rescuer or the cause of the twist.
- **Turn the wrong into a win:** micro-dramas set up a humiliation, then pay it off with vindication.

## End
- **End on the emotional peak, then cut fast:** 2-5 s from the payoff to the last frame (MrBeast memo; Hoyos: "if you linger after the high note, people won't re-watch").
- **The end writes the memory** (peak-end rule, Kahneman et al. 1993). Tension in the middle, resolution at the end (System1).
- **CTA both audible and visible, at 93-97%.** All 6 famous ads put the logo at 92-97%. CTA cards: +45% recall (TikTok).
- **Call back to the hook in the last line.**

## Length
- **40-50 s works when it builds to a late peak** (Baumgartner et al.). TikTok's best conversion window is 21-34 s (+280%), so also cut a 21-34 s version: hook, one re-hook, the reveal, the CTA.

## Zack D. Films top 20 story shorts (measured 2026-10-06; 70.5M to 123.7M views each)

| Measure | Median (range) |
|---|---|
| Length | 31.7 s (21.7 to 61.2) |
| Voice starts | 0.10 s |
| Hook sentence | 4.6 s, 14 words |
| First complication | 6.4 s (16.6%) |
| New beat | every ~3.5 s, never more than ~6 s apart |
| Mid peak | 47.8% |
| Turn | 61.6% (13 of 20), or early at 19.5% (7 of 20) |
| Resolution | 69.4% |
| Last line starts | 80.3% |
| Pace | 171 wpm |

**Openings:**
- 19 of 20 open in the middle of an event, in past tense.
- 13 of 20 start with He/She/They.
- The character is named in only 1 of 20.
- 10 of 20 open on physical peril.
- 44% of the beats after the hook start with "And" or "But".

**Endings:**
- Outcome payoff (6), twist (4), open question (3), callback (2), emotional payoff (2).
- Long ones add one more twist after the rescue.

**Pictures:**
- The first frame shows what the first words name (20 of 20).
- The last frame shows the last line's noun (20 of 20).
- Shots last about 1.9 to 2.8 s.
