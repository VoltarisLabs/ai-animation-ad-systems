# Sabri Suby

**Who:** Founder of King Kong (digital agency), author of *Sell Like Crazy* (2019), Shark Tank Australia investor. Reports $300M+ of Meta ad spend managed.
**Source:** his YouTube channel, youtube.com/@SabriSubyOfficial (470 videos + 336 shorts). This file is built from full transcripts, scanned 2026-09-26.
**Coverage so far:** 42 of 470 long-form videos read in full. Updated as more are read; see [../transcripts/youtube-channel-scan-log.md](../transcripts/youtube-channel-scan-log.md).
**Core idea:** An ad's only job is to sell the click. Win it with a pattern interrupt, burning intrigue and a big specific benefit, then let an offer that is hard to refuse do the selling.

Video IDs below are `youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`. Numbers he quotes (split-test ratios, lifts) are his own claims, not independent evidence.

## 1. Frameworks

### The hyper-dopamine ad
**Pattern interrupt (visual) + burning intrigue + big specific benefit.** He repeats this in at least five videos (`PB6Z96hTQWk`, `Ea1hFxPx3JA`, `QN_LVgvik-k`, `hASHO5ap1Sw`, `plB3mEMLGag`).
- **Pattern match:** show something the viewer already links to their world, so they know it's for them.
- **Pattern break:** show something strange enough to stop the thumb.
- Layer interrupts through the whole ad, not only in the first frame, because attention drops again mid-ad.
- **Intrigue without a benefit is "blind curiosity."** It pulls in everyone and converts no one. His contrast: "Supermodels apply these three simple tricks to look younger" (the benefit filters the right viewer) versus a celebrity-gossip tease with no benefit (anyone clicks, nobody buys) (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`).

### Market pyramid and sophistication
- **Market pyramid:** about 3% of a market is buying now. In one version the rest splits into 17% gathering information, 20% problem aware and 60% not yet aware (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`). Most businesses fight over the 3%. The bigger money is in reaching the rest before they search.
- **Red, orange and blue ocean** (`oEVLJsgQg0s`, `XW-Ut86xf5g`):
  - **Red:** actively searching. Responds to the offer, price and terms.
  - **Orange:** problem aware. Responds to a claim plus proof, and the mechanism does the work.
  - **Blue:** unaware. Responds only to story and explanation.

### Halo Strategy (research before writing)
Fill a worksheet of the market's pains, fears, hopes and dreams **in their exact words** (`plB3mEMLGag`, `hASHO5ap1Sw`, `PB6Z96hTQWk`).
- **Sources:** long, emotional Reddit and Quora comments; large niche Facebook groups; a forum-only Google filter; and your own support emails summarised by an LLM.
- **Rule:** paste their words; do not paraphrase them. The aim is to join the conversation already running in the buyer's head.

### Godfather Offer (`OE4u-PFYSns`)
An offer so good they can't refuse it. His line: "a compelling offer is 10 times more powerful than a convincing argument."
1. List what you sell.
2. Separately, research what the market is starving for.
3. If there's a gap, change the offer, not the market.
4. Draft the most generous version you can imagine, then wind it back to something you can actually deliver.

The parts:
- **Rationale:** why the offer is this good. Without a reason, people assume a catch.
- **Built-up value:** itemise every component.
- **Price:** set against that value.
- **Premiums (bonuses):** two or three is his sweet spot.
- **Power guarantee:** move the risk onto the business.
- **Scarcity:** real limits only. He warns that fake scarcity gets spotted fast.

His test: if the offer doesn't keep the founder up at night, it isn't strong enough.

### Hero mechanism (`IBjTr6ILJF0`)
The unique method that explains why this works when everything else failed. Build it from three questions:
1. Why do they have the problem?
2. What have they already tried?
3. Why did that fall short?

Name it, and attribute it to a credible or relatable source.

### Magic Lantern
Map every step to the outcome and give the early steps away as free value before the offer. The offer starts the sale; the free value keeps it going (`mOQbtHp3WBU`, `oEVLJsgQg0s`).

### 17-step sales message (given three times: `YTK4kXzrmzE`, `zpTsCrOvGao`, `HsjuAvqs3zU`)
Call out the audience → big-benefit headline → sub-headline that backs it up → intrigue bullets → shine a light on the problem → the solution → credentials → benefits → social proof → the offer → bonuses → value stack → price → real scarcity → guarantee → a CTA that tells rather than asks → a warning plus a PS.

### Nine questions every funnel must answer (`oEVLJsgQg0s`)
1. Is this for me?
2. Why now?
3. Is it the right solution, compared with what I've tried?
4. What's new about it?
5. What's the proof?
6. How does it work?
7. What do others say?
8. What's the guarantee?
9. What do I do next?

### Barbell creative (`QN_LVgvik-k`)
Run ultra-raw "ghetto statics" (unedited phone photos) at one end and occasional big productions at the other. Skip the crowded middle of templated UGC and branded statics, which he says is where returns collapse.

## 2. Actionable rules
1. **Sell the click, not the product.** Trying to close inside the ad is the "cardinal sin" (`PB6Z96hTQWk`, `NTmWqnPIb2s`).
2. **If it looks like an ad, it's dead on arrival.** Native, personal, phone-camera looks beat polish (`NTmWqnPIb2s`, `QN_LVgvik-k`).
3. **Lead with the benefit.** He says positive, big-benefit hooks beat pain-avoidance hooks about 8 times in 10 (his claim). Agitate the pain deeper in the copy, not in the hook, and still test both (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`).
4. **Drop them into the high point, not the setup.** Replacing a long benefit lead-in with "I got 72 leads in 5 days. WTF?" is credited with a 5x ROAS lift (his claim) (`QN_LVgvik-k`).
5. **Specific beats general.** General claims raise doubt. A precise, smaller, first-person number beats a big round one (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`, `K9kYwUWUjLc`).
6. **Name what they already tried and why it fell short,** or you are one more indistinguishable voice (`plB3mEMLGag`, `oEVLJsgQg0s`).
7. **Answer the question behind the search.** For "divorce lawyer," his ad answers what most of those searchers are actually worried about, rather than repeating the keyword (`7xtRHa7b96w`).
8. **Write at a 4th to 5th grade reading level.** Short words, sentences and paragraphs. Write "we" and "I," never the brand name in the third person (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`, `wzPQu8FlXlM`).
9. **Consumption comes before conversion.** Clutter, timers, extra fonts and early asks stop people reading before any persuasion can work. Strip the page down (`K9kYwUWUjLc`).
10. **"Learn More" is his best-performing CTA.** A soft ask moves more people to the page where the selling happens (`QN_LVgvik-k`).
11. **The creative is the targeting now.** Use broad targeting plus hyper-specific copy, not stacked interests (`NTmWqnPIb2s`).
12. **Don't kill a fading ad too early.** Judge it on 7-day rolling data. Check frequency (under 3 means it isn't fatigued) and whether costs rose across the whole account. Refresh the creative first, then the headline, then the body copy (`QN_LVgvik-k`).
13. **Volume:** write several new statics a day, shoot 10 to 12 hooks per campaign, and always have at least 3 split tests running (source video not logged).
14. **Keep a swipe file** of anything that "winks at you" (`wzPQu8FlXlM`, `hASHO5ap1Sw`).
15. **Obsess over people, not algorithms.** Platforms change and psychology doesn't (`PB6Z96hTQWk`).
16. **80% of copywriting is knowing what to say, 20% is how to say it** (`EdTar2tmqfw`).

## 3. Psychological triggers
- **Curiosity gap + benefit** (matches "Curiosity gap").
- **Pattern interrupt / pattern match.** NEW (practitioner).
- **Reason why:** state the rationale behind a strong offer (matches "Reason why").
- **Risk reversal:** the guarantee (matches "Loss aversion" and "Risk reversal").
- **Anchoring and three tiers:** anchor on the top tier; the middle tier sells (matches "Anchoring" and "Contrast") (source video not logged).
- **Storytelling over specs:** in his luxury-car teardown, the Bentley rep sold the story of the stitching rather than the horsepower (`SBkY-hQyY2U`).
- **Confident price statement:** state the price calmly, then stay silent (`SBkY-hQyY2U`, `rsgvkkpNg7Y`).
- **Tribe and identity:** ownership as a signal of who you are (matches "Identity / future self") (source video not logged).

Luxury-brand tactics he documents but that **we do not use**, because they rely on deception or pressure: invented stock shortages (Rolex, Lamborghini), deliberately rude staff (McLaren), hiding prices to control the conversation, and "missing piece" upsells that create the pain first (Apple) (`ZCw6BhJVe3k`, `WKbh5AQoqaE`, `SBkY-hQyY2U`, `0eHXAcHpakY`). He himself says scarcity has to be real.

## 4. Examples and teardowns
- **His agency ad** (he credits it with $34M; unverified) opens by naming a rival's hollow promise (a new logo and more exposure), then describes the marketing budget being wiped out six months later (`plB3mEMLGag`). It destroys an alternative the viewer has already heard, instead of stating a generic pain.
- **"This client getting hacked feels illegal,"** said while he holds up a USB stick (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`). "Illegal" implies forbidden information, and the object opens a question the line doesn't answer.
- **A HiSmile ad reads like tabloid gossip** ("She was skeptical. Wait until you see her results.") and uses an awkward mid-use product shot instead of the category's default smiling face (`Ea1hFxPx3JA`).
- **Weak vs fixed, from a funnel teardown** (`K9kYwUWUjLc`): "Grow your first 100k followers" has no mechanism, no timeframe and nothing different. The fix leads with an unusual credential and a specific, provable, first-person result.
- **Trojan story** (`QN_LVgvik-k`): unrelated human drama earns the read, then bridges to the product. It's a technique, **not** one to use on painful real situations such as losing a home.

## Apply it (example: local home-buyer ads)
Use only terms you can confirm. No numbers, speed claims or scarcity you can't back up.
- **Many hook banks lean negative** ("Stop / Don't" commands). By his rule, test benefit-led versions head to head. For example, "Sell it with the furniture still in it" against "Stop cleaning it out."
- **Halo Strategy fixes a common gap:** research that never reaches the lines. Paste sellers' exact Reddit and forum words into the hook and the pain section, unedited.
- **Name the alternatives they already considered** (listing with an agent, fixing it first, doing nothing) and contrast them with confirmed facts only: no commission, no repairs, belongings can stay, they pick the date.
- **Godfather Offer, honest version.** Rationale: we buy the house ourselves. Value: everything they no longer have to do. Guarantee: no obligation to accept. Scarcity: none, so leave it out.
- **Look native.** A calm person on a phone camera, on a personal-looking page. No broker-style graphics.
- **Soft CTA:** "Learn More," or "See what we'd offer. No obligation." That is also the most literally accurate wording.
- **Short and direct for searchers** (red ocean). **Story and explanation for heirs** who haven't started looking yet (blue ocean).
