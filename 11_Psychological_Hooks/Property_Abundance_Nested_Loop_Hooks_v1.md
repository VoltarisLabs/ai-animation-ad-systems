# Property Abundance: Nested-Loop Hook Scripts v1

**Status (2026-09-29): legacy, superseded.** Written before the copywriting playbook. The current Ad 16 deliverable is `03_Ad_Scripts_and_Briefs/Property_Abundance_Ad16_JMSN_Breakdown_v3.md` (script and scene breakdown, benefit-led hook, catch said first). This file's scores use the old 5-axis rubric, not the playbook's 7-point score; several hooks here failed the newer kill checks (announce-style "Stop/Don't" commands). Kept for its test-control hooks and the nested-loop worked examples. Note also: Scripts 1-2 depend on "we show you the math" (unconfirmed) and Script 3 on buying occupied properties (unconfirmed), so none of these scripts can run as written.

*Written 2026-09-24. Untested.*

**Structure:** Open A → Open B → Open C → Payload → Close C → Close B → Close A → CTA.

**Built from:**
- `04_Audience_Research/Buyer_Avatar/README.md` and `Psychological_Profile_and_Hook_Map.md`: each buyer's wound, objection, levers, trigger moments and hook killers.
- `05_Tutorials/Hook_Psychology_Research_Report.md`: the nested-loop method and the evidence behind it.

## Rules every script follows

- **A = the hook.** It hits the avatar's core wound or hidden desire. A closes **last**, and the brand appears there.
- **B = the main objection**, said out loud.
- **C = a belief break**, answered by the payload (the mechanism).
- **Every close starts with "That's why…"** and repeats the words of its open, so the answer clearly lands on the question. This is the pattern from Howie's "edible deodorant" slide.
- **Payload between the steps**, not only in the middle (Howie's updated slide 22).
- **Length:** about 60-75 s. Howie: 30 s is too short for three loops.
- **The three gates** (from the Hook Map):
  - Situation: the right person recognises themselves in under 1 s.
  - Predator: no line the scammer they fear could have written. No "top dollar", no urgency, never "distressed".
  - Truth: no unconfirmed claim.
- **Narrator speaks to "you".** No AI character plays a customer (FTC 2024 rule on fake testimonials).
- **[claim-dependent]** marks a line that is still a PLACEHOLDER in the research file. Confirm it with the business or cut the line.

## Script 1: Emily "The Heir" (Overwhelmed Inheritor)

**Core wound:** grief dressed up as logistics.
**Hidden desire:** permission to stop.

| Step | Line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Open A** | "Keeping your parent's house isn't how you honor them." | Absolution, her #1 lever. It says what she thinks and won't admit. "Keeping vs honoring" is an odd pairing, which opens the loop. |
| Payload | "Fourth trip this month. For a house nobody lives in. The closet's still full." | Two trigger moments: the drive and the closet. |
| **Open B** | "And the question you're scared to ask: am I getting ripped off because I just want it gone?" | Her primary objection, word for word. Naming it first disarms it. |
| Payload | "Your sibling won't pay for repairs, and won't take an offer." | Family-conflict trigger. |
| **Open C** | "Here's the honest part: a cash offer is lower than a listed sale." | Self-incrimination, the one move that works on all three avatars. It opens "then why take it?" |
| **Payload** | "A listed sale means repairs, a clean-out, showings, and months of taxes while it sits. Four hours away." | The mechanism. Every item is a task she wants removed. |
| **Close C** | "That's why the lower number can still leave you with more: no repairs, no clean-out, no more months of taxes." [claim-dependent] | Answers C. Repeats "lower". |
| **Close B** | "That's why you're not getting ripped off, if you can see how the number is built. We show you the math first." [claim-dependent] | Answers B. Showing the maths is her trust lever. |
| **Close A** | "Keeping it was never how you honor them. Letting it go, without a fight, is. This is Property Abundance." | Repeats A. Gives her permission, "without a fight" covers the family problem, and the brand comes last. |
| CTA | "Get the number and the math. Show your family. Decide together." | A line she can take to her family. No pressure. |

**[claim-dependent] Confirm:** as-is purchase, no clean-out, offer maths shown to the seller.

## Script 2: Marcus "The Cornered Homeowner" (Financially Cornered)

**Core wound:** shame.
**Hidden desire:** a calm map of his options.

| Step | Line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Open A** | "Being behind on your house still leaves you options. Only one of them is us." | Calm and non-judging. It maps his options, and "only one is us" volunteers that you're not the only answer. He wonders what the others are. |
| Payload | "You looked this up at 2 a.m. Nobody has to know." | 2 a.m. search trigger, plus privacy. |
| **Open B** | "And you're right to ask why a cash buyer is talking to you at all." | His objection: "you're taking advantage because I'm in trouble." Agreeing with his distrust earns trust. |
| **Open C** | "The letter you got isn't the deadline you think it is." | Time stated as a fact, not pressure. Breaks his panic belief. |
| **Payload** | "Foreclosure is a process with steps and dates, and the dates depend on your state. A HUD-approved housing counselor will tell you yours, for free." | Specific and verifiable. His file says specificity beats emotion, and it recommends counselors. |
| **Close C** | "That's why the letter isn't your deadline. Your state's dates are, and now you know who to ask." | Answers C. Repeats "letter" and "deadline". |
| **Close B** | "That's why we tell you how we make money: we buy below retail, and we show you exactly how the number is built." [claim-dependent] | Answers B. Saying the margin out loud is his biggest disarm. |
| **Close A** | "So being behind still leaves you options: ask your lender about a modification, list it, or sell it as it is. Only one of them is us. This is Property Abundance." [claim-dependent] | Repeats A exactly. Control goes back to him. |
| CTA | "If selling fits, we'll give you a number. If it doesn't, we'll tell you." [claim-dependent] | Answers "what happens if I say no?" with a plain exit. |

**[claim-dependent] Confirm:** as-is purchase, offer maths shown, no-obligation, and that the business will tell sellers when selling doesn't fit.

## Script 3: Jason "The Burned-Out Landlord"

**Core wound:** made a mark by the system.
**Hidden desire:** finality, today.

| Step | Line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Open A** | "The tenant can stay. The problem doesn't have to stay yours." [claim-dependent] | Qualifies him in the first second: "tenant" is the hook. The paradox opens "how?" |
| Payload | "Rent day was Tuesday. Nothing came in. Again." | Rent-day trigger. Kept short, because his hook window is the shortest. |
| **Open B** | "Winning the eviction isn't the same as winning." | Permission to stop. It flips the fight he's in. |
| Payload | "The hearing got pushed. Third time. The attorney's invoice came anyway." | Two trigger moments. |
| **Open C** | "The rent you're chasing isn't the number that's hurting you." | He thinks in monthly loss. Opens "then what number?" |
| **Payload** | "Add it up: the mortgage out of your own paycheck, the attorney, the months with no rent, the damage you'll find when they leave." | He does the maths himself (self-referencing). No invented figures. |
| **Close C** | "That's why the rent isn't the number that hurts. What hurts is what it costs you every month you keep it." | Answers C. Repeats "the number". |
| **Close B** | "That's why winning the eviction isn't winning: even if you win, you've paid for months to get back a house that still costs you." | Answers B. Repeats "winning". |
| **Close A** | "So the tenant can stay, and the problem stops being yours the day you sign. This is Property Abundance." [claim-dependent] | Repeats A. Delivers the finality he wants. |
| CTA | "Get a number. Then decide." | His own words, with no process language. |

**[claim-dependent] Confirm:** buys occupied properties. **Without this, Script 3 cannot run.**

## Before shooting

1. Confirm every [claim-dependent] claim with the business, or cut the line.
2. Score each Open A with the Hook Map rubric (5 axes, 0-2 each). Under 7, rewrite.
3. **Captions:** 2-3 keywords per line, not full sentences. Full text that repeats the voice lowered recall in research.
4. **Test:** cut one version with nested loops and one with the same lines in plain order. Compare 3-second hold and average watch time.
