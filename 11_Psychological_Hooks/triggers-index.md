# Psychological Triggers in Copy

Each trigger comes with why it works, how to write it, example lines, and how it backfires. They are grouped by the job they do in an ad: **Stop** the scroll, make them **Believe**, make them **Want**, get them to **Act**.

Example lines are original and rotate across industries. `[brackets]` = fill in.

**Evidence:** **Strong** = widely replicated in research. **Moderate** = supported but depends on context. **Practitioner** = proven in ad testing by the copywriters, not formally studied. **Weak** = failed replication, so don't rely on it.

---

## STOP: earn the first 2 seconds

### Curiosity gap (Practitioner: Caples, Sugarman)
**Why:** An open question the brain wants closed.
**Write it:** Tease the answer and tie it to a benefit. Caples found curiosity alone tested weak.
> "The 12-minute workout habit that finally stuck."
> "Why your invoices get paid last (it's not the amount)."

**Backfires when:** The payoff doesn't match the tease. That's clickbait, and it lowers conversions and trust.

### Self-relevance / call-out (Practitioner: Caples, Halbert)
**Why:** People notice anything that names them, much like hearing your own name in a crowded room.
**Write it:** Name the audience or their exact situation in the first words.
> "Salon owners with empty Tuesday chairs:"
> "Inherited a house you don't want to deal with?"

**Backfires when:** It calls out a sensitive personal attribute ("Are you in debt?"). Meta can reject ads that do this.

### Pattern interrupt / novelty (Practitioner: Schwartz, Sugarman)
**Why:** The brain filters out what's expected and notices what isn't.
**Write it:** Contradict a common belief, or open with a strange specific detail.
> "Stop drinking 8 glasses of water a day."
> "Our best customer found us in a parking lot."

**Backfires when:** The contrarian claim can't be backed up.

### Cognitive ease (Strong: Kahneman)
**Why:** Text that's easy to read feels more true and gets read more.
**Write it:** Short words, one idea per line, high contrast, no jargon.
> "Clear skin. No harsh acids. 30 days." instead of "Our dermatologically formulated solution..."

**Backfires when:** Simple turns into vague. Keep it specific.

## BELIEVE: lower their skepticism

### Specificity (Practitioner + supported: Hopkins, Ogilvy, Sugarman)
**Why:** Exact numbers sound measured. Round numbers sound made up.
**Write it:** Replace every adjective with a number, time, place or name.
> "Set up in 11 minutes" instead of "quick setup."
> "Closed in 9 days" instead of "fast closing."

**Backfires when:** The number isn't real. Only use numbers you can prove.

### Social proof (Strong: Cialdini)
**Why:** When unsure, people copy others, especially others like them.
**Write it:** Proof from people who share the reader's role, place or problem.
> "312 Ohio landlords used this last year."
> "Rated 4.9 by 1,200 new moms."

**Backfires when:** You highlight that "most people fail at X." That makes failing seem normal. Show that most people *do* the right thing.

### Authority (Moderate: Cialdini)
**Why:** People defer to credible experts.
**Write it:** Credentials that matter to *this* reader, stated briefly.
> "From the dentist who's placed 4,000 implants."
> "Licensed, insured, 22 years in [city]."

**Backfires when:** Credentials are irrelevant or overdone. Authority should support the promise, not replace it.

### Honest admission / "damaging admission" (Practitioner: Halbert, Sugarman)
**Why:** Admitting a small flaw makes every other claim more believable.
**Write it:** Admit something true and minor, then turn it into a reason to buy.
> "We're not the cheapest. We're the ones who show up on time."
> "It's not for beginners. It's for people who already tried the basics."

**Backfires when:** The flaw is a real deal-breaker.

### Reason why / "because" (Moderate: Langer study; Practitioner: Hopkins)
**Why:** A request with a reason gets more yeses, even when the reason is small.
**Write it:** Explain why the offer, price or deadline exists.
> "We're opening 20 spots because we just hired two new techs."
> "We can pay cash because we buy with our own money, not a bank's."

**Backfires when:** The reason is obviously invented.

### Mechanism (Practitioner: Schwartz)
**Why:** In crowded markets, people stop believing results but still believe *how* something works.
**Write it:** Name the method and explain it in one line.
> "Our 2-step sealing method stops the leak at the source, not the stain."
> "It builds your workout around your calendar, not the other way around."

**Backfires when:** The mechanism is jargon. It has to be easy to picture.

### Unity / shared identity (Moderate: Cialdini)
**Why:** People favor "one of us."
**Write it:** Speak from inside the group.
> "From one nurse to another:"
> "Built by founders who hated bookkeeping too."

**Backfires when:** The identity is faked. The audience can tell.

## WANT: make the result feel necessary

### Loss aversion (Strong: Kahneman and Tversky)
**Why:** Losses feel about twice as strong as equal gains.
**Write it:** Frame the cost of waiting or doing nothing, and make it accurate.
> "Every month you wait costs you about $300 in missed write-offs."
> "That small leak is rotting the wood every time it rains."

**Backfires when:** It's exaggerated. Fear that's too strong gets ads rejected and makes readers tune out.

### Identity / future self (Practitioner: Schwartz; Moderate: Cialdini)
**Why:** People buy who they want to become, not the product.
**Write it:** Describe the person they become, not the features.
> "For the parent who wants to be the one who keeps up at the park."
> "Be the landlord who never gets the 2am call."

**Backfires when:** It sounds like a slogan. Keep it concrete.

### Vivid picture / involvement (Practitioner: Sugarman, Hoke's 4 Ps)
**Why:** Imagining owning it creates a sense of ownership before the purchase.
**Write it:** Put them inside the result with sensory detail.
> "Picture Monday morning with every invoice already paid."
> "Imagine opening the door to a house that's already sold."

**Backfires when:** It goes on too long. One sentence is enough.

### Anchoring (Strong: Kahneman and Tversky, Ariely)
**Why:** The first number seen sets the scale for every number after it.
**Write it:** Show the bigger number first: the regular price, the cost of the alternative, or the cost of the problem.
> "Replacing the roof: $14,000. Fixing the leak now: $400."
> "A bookkeeper costs $800 a month. This costs $29."

**Backfires when:** The anchor is fake. False "regular prices" violate consumer law.

### Contrast (Moderate: Ariely's decoy effect)
**Why:** People judge options by comparing them, not in isolation.
**Write it:** Put a clear before/after or us/them side by side.
> "Them: a monthly report. Us: booked calls."
> "Before: buckets in the attic. After: one dry, quiet house."

**Backfires when:** The comparison is unfair or names a competitor without legal clearance.

## ACT: get the click now

### Scarcity (Moderate: Cialdini, cookie-jar study)
**Why:** Things seem more valuable when they're limited or going away.
**Write it:** State real limits and the reason for them.
> "We take 5 new clients a month. 2 spots left for October."
> "The founding price ends when the first 100 sign up."

**Backfires when:** It's fake. Fake scarcity breaks ad policies and the FTC Act, and it destroys trust once people notice.

### Urgency (Practitioner + Moderate)
**Why:** A deadline turns "someday" into "now."
**Write it:** Tie the deadline to something real.
> "Enroll by Thursday. The cohort starts Monday."
> "Book before the first freeze and we'll inspect for free."

**Backfires when:** There's a countdown that resets. People notice.

### Zero price / free first step (Moderate: Ariely)
**Why:** "Free" is treated as its own category, not just a low price.
**Write it:** Make the first step free and say exactly what they get.
> "Free 15-minute roof check. No obligation."
> "Your first week is free. Cancel in one tap."

**Backfires when:** "Free" hides a catch. Say what's included.

### Reciprocity (Strong: Cialdini)
**Why:** People feel they should return a favor.
**Write it:** Give something useful first, then ask.
> "Here are 3 signs your water heater is failing. If you spot 2, we'll check it free."
> "Grab the free checklist. No email needed."

**Backfires when:** The "gift" is just a pitch in disguise.

### Commitment / small first step (Moderate to strong: Cialdini; Fogg)
**Why:** People keep acting in line with small steps they've already taken. Easy actions happen more.
**Write it:** Ask for the smallest possible step, in the first person.
> "Answer 3 questions to see your price."
> "Tap to get my free quote."

**Backfires when:** The "small step" leads to a long form. Keep the promise.

### Defaults / recommended option (Strong: Thaler)
**Why:** Most people pick the pre-selected or recommended choice.
**Write it:** Tell them what most people choose.
> "Most homeowners pick the full inspection."
> "Our most popular plan: [name]."

**Backfires when:** The default serves you, not them.

### Risk reversal (Practitioner: Halbert, Sugarman)
**Why:** It removes the fear of making a mistake, which is often the last thing stopping a purchase.
**Write it:** A clear guarantee, stated right next to the call to action.
> "If it breaks you out, we refund the whole bottle, even if it's empty."
> "Miss the date, and your next month is free."

**Backfires when:** The guarantee has fine print that contradicts it.

## Stacking triggers in one short ad
One from each job is usually enough. More than that feels like manipulation. (Numbers below are placeholders; use your own proof.)

| Job | Pick one | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Stop | Call-out | "Salon owners with empty Tuesday chairs:" |
| Believe | Specificity + social proof | "140 salons filled them in 30 days with one text." |
| Want | Loss aversion | "Every empty chair is about $90 gone." |
| Act | Free first step | "Get the free text template." |

## Don't use
| Claim | Why |
|---|---|
| Social priming (e.g., words that "make you walk slower") | Failed replication. Kahneman acknowledged it |
| "Croc brain" / reptile-brain targeting | Based on the triune brain model, which neuroscience has dropped |
| Single retracted or disputed studies (e.g., the 2012 honesty-pledge paper) | Retracted in 2021 over fabricated data |
