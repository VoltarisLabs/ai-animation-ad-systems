# Psychological Triggers Index

Each trigger, who documented it, how reliable the evidence is, and how to use it in an ad.
Evidence: **Strong** = widely replicated. **Moderate** = supported but context-dependent. **Weak** = replication problems or mostly anecdotal practitioner wisdom.

| Trigger | Source | Evidence | Ad use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss aversion | Kahneman & Tversky | Strong | Frame the cost of waiting as a loss |
| Anchoring | Kahneman & Tversky, Ariely | Strong | Show the reference price first |
| Framing | Kahneman & Tversky | Strong | Test gain vs. loss wording of the same fact |
| Defaults | Thaler & Sunstein | Strong | Pre-select the recommended option |
| Social proof | Cialdini | Strong | Specific, local, similar-to-me proof |
| Reciprocity | Cialdini | Strong | Give value before asking |
| Commitment / consistency | Cialdini | Moderate to strong | Tiny first step, then the bigger ask |
| Scarcity | Cialdini | Moderate | Only real limits; loss of access is strongest |
| Authority | Cialdini | Moderate | Credentials, expert on camera |
| Liking / similarity | Cialdini | Moderate | Real person, audience lookalike |
| Unity | Cialdini | Moderate | Shared identity ("one of us") |
| Decoy effect | Ariely et al. | Moderate | 3 tiers with an inferior decoy |
| Zero-price effect | Ariely et al. | Moderate | "Free" for the entry step |
| Endowment / ownership | Thaler | Strong | Trials, "your custom quote" |
| Mental accounting | Thaler | Moderate | "$2 a day," pay with a windfall |
| Peak-end rule | Kahneman | Moderate | Make the last funnel step feel great |
| Cognitive ease | Kahneman | Strong | Simple, legible, repeated message |
| Friction / ability | Fogg | Strong in practice | Fewer fields, one-tap CTA |
| Curiosity gap | Sugarman, Caples | Practitioner | Pair it with a benefit, never alone |
| Specificity | Hopkins, Ogilvy, Sugarman | Practitioner, supported | Exact numbers beat round claims |
| Reason why ("because") | Hopkins, Halbert, Langer study | Moderate | Explain why the offer exists |
| Damaging admission | Halbert | Practitioner | Admit a small flaw to earn belief |
| Identification / identity | Schwartz, Cialdini (Unity) | Practitioner | Speak to who they want to be |
| New mechanism | Schwartz | Practitioner | Explain *how* it works differently |
| Disgust / gross reveal | Stefan Georgi | Practitioner | Open on the ugly thing (the hole, the leak), then explain |
| Testable self-proof | Stefan Georgi (hook type 15) | Practitioner | A check the viewer can do right now on their own situation |
| Emotional-temperature congruence | Stefan Georgi | Practitioner | Landing page and first reply open at the same heat as the ad |
| Dimensionalization | Stefan Georgi | Practitioner, supported (specificity) | Turn the category word into the exact scene |
| Pattern interrupt / pattern match | Sabri Suby | Practitioner | Strange visual to stop the scroll, or a familiar one that says "this is for you" |
| Benefit-first framing | Sabri Suby | Practitioner (his split tests) | Lead with the gain; keep pain agitation for the body |
| Social priming (e.g., words that "make you walk slower") | Kahneman (ch. 4) | **Weak, failed replication** | Do not rely on it |

## Compliance guardrails
- Fake scarcity or fake countdown timers can break Meta/Google ad policies and FTC rules.
- Testimonials and reviews must be real and represent typical results.
- Loss framing must be accurate. Exaggerated fear claims get ads rejected.
