# Offer Builder

A great ad cannot save a weak offer (Halbert). Build the offer before writing the copy.

## 1. The value equation
Alex Hormozi (*$100M Offers*) sums up perceived value as:

```
Value = (Dream outcome x Perceived likelihood of success)
        / (Time until result x Effort and sacrifice)
```

Raise the top, shrink the bottom. Score your offer 1 to 5 on each part:

| Lever | Question | Ways to improve it |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Is the result something they badly want, in their words? | Name the end state, not the feature |
| Likelihood | Do they believe it will work *for them*? | Proof from similar people, guarantee, a mechanism that explains why |
| Time | How fast do they see the first result? | Quick first win, a faster path, "done in 7 days" |
| Effort | How much work, cost or discomfort? | Done-for-you, templates, fewer steps, no-risk first step |

Any lever scoring 3 or less is the weakest part of the offer. Fix that before writing.

## 2. Offer stack checklist
- [ ] **Core deliverable**, stated as a result.
- [ ] **First step** that asks little of them: free, cheap or fast (entry-point offer, DigitalMarketer).
- [ ] **Bonuses** that each remove one objection or effort. A bonus should answer "but what about...?"
- [ ] **Guarantee** (pick one that is true and sustainable):
  - Unconditional ("money back, no questions, 30 days")
  - Conditional ("do X, and if you don't get Y, we refund")
  - Performance ("we work free until you hit Y")
  - Service ("if we miss the date, the next month is free")
- [ ] **Reason why** the offer exists: launch, slow season, testing a new service, capacity.
- [ ] **Real scarcity or urgency**: limited slots, a deadline tied to something real, a price increase date.
- [ ] **Anchor**: show the regular price, the cost of the alternative, or the cost of doing nothing, before your price.
- [ ] **Name** the offer, e.g. "The 7-Day Website Rebuild." A named offer feels more tangible and gives the ad a new thing to talk about.

## 3. Pricing presentation
- Three tiers, with the middle one as the target and a top or decoy tier that makes it look like the best deal (Ariely).
- Break the price into the smallest honest unit: "$3 a day," "less than one missed client" (Thaler, mental accounting).
- "Free" beats any small price for an entry step (zero-price effect).

## 4. Offer to ad handoff
Write one sentence before any copy:
> "We help [audience] get [dream outcome] in [time] without [effort or fear], guaranteed by [guarantee]."

If you can't fill it in, the offer isn't ready.
