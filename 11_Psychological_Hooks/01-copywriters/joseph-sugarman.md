# Joseph Sugarman

**Who:** Direct marketer behind JS&A (mail-order electronics) and BluBlocker sunglasses. Known for editorial-style long print ads.
**Key works:** *Advertising Secrets of the Written Word* (1998), later *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* (2006), *Triggers*.
**Core idea:** Every element of an ad exists to get the next element read.

## 1. Frameworks
- **The Slippery Slide.** The headline gets the first sentence read. The first sentence gets the second read, and so on. A reader who slides all the way down is ready to buy.
- **Seeds of curiosity.** End paragraphs with a short line that pulls into the next one ("But there's more." / "Here's why.").
- **Selling a concept, not a product.** People buy the idea and feeling. The product is how they get there.
- **Psychological triggers** (he catalogued about 30). A selection useful for ads:
  consistency, involvement/ownership, honesty, credibility, proof of value, justify the purchase, greed, authority, satisfaction conviction, linking, familiarity, hope, guilt, specificity, storytelling, desire to belong, desire to collect, urgency, fear, instant gratification, exclusivity, simplicity, raising objections.

## 2. Actionable rules
1. Make the first sentence very short, so it is easy to start reading.
2. Every line should make the reader want to read the next line.
3. Raise the objection yourself, then answer it. Unspoken objections kill sales.
4. Use specific numbers ("97.3%" beats "almost all").
5. Help the reader imagine owning it (involvement): describe using it.
6. Give the logical justification after the emotional pull. People buy on emotion and justify with logic.
7. Make it simple to buy. Complexity loses sales.
8. Use a strong guarantee to remove risk.

## 3. Psychological triggers
See the list above. Most applicable in short ads: curiosity, specificity, involvement, objection-raising, urgency, simplicity.

## 4. Examples
- **BluBlocker:** Ads and TV spots put the glasses on ordinary people and let them react on the spot. The copy sold the experience of seeing through them, not the lens spec. Lesson: demonstration plus involvement.
- **Editorial-style layouts:** Ads that look like articles lowered the "this is an ad" defense and made readers go further.

## Apply it
Read your ad line by line and ask, "Does this line make them read the next?" Cut every line that does not.
