# Eugene Schwartz

**Who:** Direct-response copywriter and publisher.
**Key work:** *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966).
**Core idea:** Copy cannot create desire. It can only find a desire that already exists in the market and aim it at your product.

## 1. Frameworks

### The 5 Levels of Awareness
Where the prospect is decides how the ad should open.

| Level | Prospect knows | Headline strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Most Aware | Your product, and wants it | Lead with the offer: product, price, deal |
| Product-Aware | Your product, not yet convinced | Prove it is better, stack proof, handle objections |
| Solution-Aware | The result they want, not your product | Lead with the result, then introduce your product as the way to get it |
| Problem-Aware | They have a problem, not the solution | Name the problem sharply, then bring in the solution |
| Unaware | Nothing yet | Open with identity, story or curiosity, never the product |

The less aware the market, the longer and more indirect the copy has to be.

### The 5 Stages of Market Sophistication
How many competing claims the market has already heard.

1. **First to market:** Make the simple, direct claim.
2. **Claim has been copied:** Make it bigger or more extreme.
3. **Claims are tired:** Introduce a new *mechanism*, the new way it works.
4. **Mechanisms are copied:** Improve or extend the mechanism: faster, easier, more complete.
5. **Market no longer believes anything:** Stop leading with claims. Lead with identification with the prospect: who they are, what they want to become.

### Mass desire
Every product rides on a desire that already exists in the market. Judge it by **urgency** (how intense), **staying power** (how long it lasts) and **scope** (how many people share it).

### Intensifying the desire (headline and body techniques)
- **Intensification:** Make the benefit more vivid, concrete and immediate.
- **Identification:** Let the reader recognize themselves or the person they want to be.
- **Gradualization:** Get agreement on believable steps before the big claim.
- **Redefinition:** Recast a drawback so it no longer counts against you.
- **Mechanization:** Show *how* it works so the claim feels real.
- **Concentration:** Point out the competing solution's weakness.
- **Camouflage:** Present the ad in a format the reader already trusts, such as editorial.

## 2. Actionable rules
1. Diagnose awareness and sophistication *before* writing the headline.
2. Never try to argue someone into a desire they do not have.
3. In crowded markets, sell the mechanism, not the result everyone else also promises.
4. The headline's job is to get the right prospect to read the next line, not to sell everything.
5. The unaware need a longer indirect path. The most aware need only the offer.
6. Write in timed, focused sessions. He reportedly worked in 33-minute blocks.

## 3. Psychological triggers
Existing desire, self-identification, belief-building by small agreements, novelty of mechanism, and future-self identity.

## 4. Examples
- A tired diet or roofing market ("save money," "best quality") is Stage 4 to 5. Winning angles are a new mechanism ("the 2-layer inspection most roofers skip") or identity ("for homeowners who want it done once").
- Retargeting audiences are Product-Aware to Most Aware. Show the offer and proof, not education.

## Apply it
Label every ad and audience with an awareness level. Cold traffic ads usually fail because they are written for Most Aware buyers.
