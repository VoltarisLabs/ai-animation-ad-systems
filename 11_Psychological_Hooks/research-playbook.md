# Research Playbook: Get Their Words Into the Lines

Most weak copy has good research behind it. The research just never reaches the lines, because the writer paraphrases it into their own voice. This file is the fix. It expands Step 2 of [AD_COPY_CHECKLIST.md](AD_COPY_CHECKLIST.md).

Sources: Sabri Suby's Halo Strategy ([sabri-suby.md](03-modern-creators/sabri-suby.md)), Stefan Georgi's RMBC research and dimensionalization ([stefan-georgi.md](03-modern-creators/stefan-georgi.md)), and Joanna Wiebe's review mining ([direct-response-copy.md](03-modern-creators/direct-response-copy.md)).

---

## 1. Mine the market's own words (60-90 minutes)
Go where people complain at length, and in private: long, emotional comments, not one-line reviews.

| Source | How to search | What to grab |
|---|---|---|
| Reddit, Quora, niche forums | `site:reddit.com "[situation]"`, or a forum-only filter such as the "Discussions" Chrome extension Sabri names | Long comments that vent, confess or ask for help |
| Facebook groups | Big groups for the situation (heirs, landlords, new parents, founders) | Posts that begin "Has anyone else..." |
| Reviews: yours and competitors' | 1-2 star reviews (what failed), 4-5 star reviews (what they hoped for) | Loves, hates, worries |
| Your own support emails, DMs, call notes | Paste into an LLM: "List the 5 most repeated pains and the 5 most repeated wants, quoting the customer's exact words" | Exact phrases with counts |
| YouTube and TikTok comments on viral videos about the problem | Sort by top comments | The one-line version people upvote |

**Rule: paste, don't paraphrase.** Put the exact words in the worksheet, typos and all. The moment you "clean them up," the punch is gone.

## 2. The worksheet (one per audience)
```
Audience + moment:     [who, in which exact situation]
Pains (their words):   "..."  "..."  "..."
Fears (their words):   "..."  "..."
Wants (their words):   "..."  "..."
What they already tried, and why it let them down:  [alternative] -> "..."
What they'd never say out loud:  [the unspoken sentence]
Words they use for the problem:  [their vocabulary, not ours]
Awareness level:       [Unaware / Problem / Solution / Product / Most aware]
```
Keep one worksheet per audience. Three audiences means three worksheets and three hook sets, not one generic set.

## 3. Map the awareness gap (Georgi)
- Estimate roughly how much of the market sits at each level. The levels overlap, and a rough guess is fine.
- **Aim at problem-aware and solution-aware for cold traffic.** Moving someone from unaware to problem-aware is the most expensive step. It's brand work.
- Read "product aware" as **category aware**: they know this kind of solution exists, not your brand. That pool is bigger.
- **The number of levels you must move them sets the length.** One level means a short ad and a direct page. Three levels means education: a longer ad, an advertorial or a two-step funnel ([offer-and-mechanism.md](offer-and-mechanism.md)).

## 4. Dimensionalize: turn the category word into the scene
Georgi's most repeated rule. Take every generic word from your draft and swap in the scene the worksheet gives you.

| Generic (what we write) | Dimensionalized (what they live) |
|---|---|
| Low energy | Sitting in the car for ten minutes before walking into the house |
| Cash-flow stress | Checking the bank app before approving payroll, twice |
| Needs repairs | The roof quote, read twice, folded into a kitchen drawer |
| Inherited property | The fourth drive this month to a house nobody lives in |
| Bad sleep | Awake at 3:12 doing tomorrow's list in your head |

**Test:** could the reader picture a specific place, object or moment? If not, it isn't dimensionalized yet.

## 5. Find the one bullseye desire
From the worksheet, pick the single want that shows up most, and phrase it the way they do. One ad or page gets one bullseye (Sabri: pitching three benefits at once dilutes all three). The other wants become other ads.

## 6. Pre-check the second click (trust engineering)
People who like your ad search your name **before** they trust your page (Sabri's "sniff test"). Before scaling spend, search your brand the way a stranger would:
- [ ] Does the website load, and does it answer "how does this work" plainly?
- [ ] Is there third-party proof (a Google Business Profile, reviews, real photos)?
- [ ] Would one bad result sit on page one unanswered?

If the second click is empty, fix it before you spend more. A great ad can't beat an empty search.

## 7. Swipe what is scaling now
- **Meta Ad Library:** search competitors and adjacent brands. An ad that has run for months is almost certainly profitable.
- **Follow the whole path:** ad, then landing page, then checkout or form. Screenshot each step.
- **Steal the structure, never the words** (Georgi). Borrow structures from unrelated niches too ("translocate"): a supplement ad's structure can carry a home-services offer.
- Save anything that "winks at you" into [swipe-file.md](swipe-file.md) with one line on why it works (Sabri).

## Done when
- [ ] A worksheet per audience, full of pasted phrases.
- [ ] An awareness level and a bullseye desire per audience.
- [ ] Ten dimensionalized scenes ready to drop into hooks and bodies.
- [ ] The second-click check passed.
