# Testing and Iteration: How Winners Are Found

Nobody writes winners on the first try. Both creators treat copy as **volume, judgment and fast feedback**: generate many, cut hard, test the hook first, and keep what the numbers keep. This file expands Step 9 of [AD_COPY_CHECKLIST.md](AD_COPY_CHECKLIST.md). The prompts are in [prompts/copy-prompts.md](prompts/copy-prompts.md).

Sources: [stefan-georgi.md](03-modern-creators/stefan-georgi.md), [sabri-suby.md](03-modern-creators/sabri-suby.md). Their numbers are their own claims.

---

## 1. Volume, then curate
- **Write 20 or more hooks per angle, keep 3 to 5.** Georgi generates in batches of 20 and keeps what he'd actually run. Sabri shoots 10 to 12 hooks per campaign so fresh openings are ready when one fatigues.
- **Rotate hook types, not just words.** Use different families from [hook-formulas.md](hook-formulas.md): contrarian, curiosity + benefit, story-in-motion, testable self-check, and so on. Nine versions of "Stop X" is one test, not nine.
- **Rotate registers.** The same brief can open as an exposé, as calm empathy, or mid-scene. Test the register as well as the wording (Georgi).
- **Test benefit-led against pain-led.** Sabri says benefit-led hooks win about 8 times in 10 (his claim). Most teams only write pain-led hooks.

## 2. Judge before you spend: the kill checks
Run these on every hook before it's scored in Step 4 of the checklist. A single "yes" means rewrite it.

| Kill check | What it catches | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Does it announce itself? ("Hey, are you...", "Today I tried...", "Hi, I'm X from Y") | No pattern interrupt; the first 2 seconds are wasted | Georgi, Sabri |
| Is it curiosity with no benefit? | "Blind curiosity": everyone clicks, nobody buys | Sabri |
| Could they solve it without clicking? | The whole mechanism is given away | Sabri |
| Does it accuse the reader? ("You don't...", "Your X is a mess") | They get defensive and scroll | Sabri |
| Could a competitor run it word for word? | Generic, not dimensionalized | Georgi |
| Does the page or body fail to pay it off? | A broken promise breeds angry, non-buying clicks | Georgi |
| Does it try to close the sale in the ad? | The ad's job is the click | Sabri |

## 3. Test order
1. **Hook first.** Keep the body identical and swap only the first 2 to 3 seconds. Hooks move results the most.
2. **Then the angle or bullseye desire.**
3. **Then the body, the offer framing and the CTA.** The unglamorous words matter too: Georgi's checkout button test found the plain word "Checkout" beat pushier options (his claim).
4. **Then format:** raw phone static vs. talking head vs. produced. Sabri's barbell: run ultra-raw and occasional big productions, and skip the templated middle.

**Test organically first when budget is tight** (Sabri). Post variants on an unbranded account and put paid spend behind the ones that hold attention.

## 4. Read results correctly
- **Hook rate is the first number to check** (Sabri): 3-second video views ÷ impressions. He calls 20-30% good and 30-40%+ exceptional (his benchmarks). When an ad fades, **re-shoot only the hook** before rebuilding the whole ad.
- **Test 3 to 5 headlines at a time,** not every variation at once, to control spend (Sabri).
- **Use 7-day rolling data,** never a single day (Sabri).
- **A fading ad isn't dead until you've checked** frequency (under about 3 is usually not fatigue) and whether costs rose across the whole account (season, news). Then refresh in order: the creative, then the headline, then the body copy (Sabri).
- **Always have at least 3 tests running** (Sabri).
- **Judge on money back, not ratios.** A lower ROAS at a bigger spend can return more cash (Sabri). For lead gen, judge on cost per qualified lead and on closed deals, not cost per click.
- **Allow for late converters.** If buyers decide weeks later, short attribution windows undercount the ad.

## 5. The AI workflow (Georgi's "copy thinker" model)
AI drafts; you judge. The writer's job is now the idea, the structure and the judgment, plus fact-checking anything the AI invents.
1. **Load context first,** then ask the model to summarise it back as a comprehension check.
2. **Fill in the brief fully.** Georgi saw output get "significantly better" once the market field was filled in. The brief comes from [research-playbook.md](research-playbook.md).
3. **Generate in batches of 20,** asking for a range of hook types and a suggested visual for each.
4. **Curate with the kill checks** above, then score with the checklist.
5. **Copy-chief the survivors** for dimensionalization and specificity. The prompt is in [prompts/copy-prompts.md](prompts/copy-prompts.md).
6. **If the model gets stuck in a style rut** (the same metaphor over and over), **open a new chat.** Don't keep correcting it in the same thread.
7. **Fact-check every number, study and claim.** Models invent them.

## 6. Two editing passes most teams skip
- **Beat-by-beat check** (Georgi): list the script's claims in order, one per line. Does any line contradict or wander from the one before ("zigzag copy")? Fix it before recording.
- **Emotional temperature check** (Georgi): read the hook, then the first line of the landing page or first reply. Same heat? A raw hook landing on clinical copy loses them.

## 7. The teardown order for any ad or funnel (Sabri)
Use it on your own work and on competitors', in this order. Small conversion tweaks come last.
1. Headline or hook: big benefit plus intrigue, or about the company?
2. Scent: does the angle hold from ad to page?
3. CTA: one clear action, repeated?
4. Proof density: enough for the size of the ask?
5. Offer strength: see [offer-and-mechanism.md](offer-and-mechanism.md).
6. Only then colours, buttons and layout.

## 8. Diagnose before you rebuild
**Three-point funnel triage** (Sabri, `zfHpRulBoSo`, `wwmh366eNPo`). When results drop, check in order:
1. Are people still clicking (same CTR and CPC)?
2. Are they still converting on the page?
3. Is the sales or close step still converting?

Fix only the step that changed.

**Other rules:**
- **Retarget by objection** (`EjgkQHEakr4`). Never repeat the original pitch to people who already passed. Guess the objection, test 2 to 3 retargeting headlines that each answer a different one, then build the full ad around the winner.
- **Founders get bored of a winner long before the market does** (`6E3th4NIoNk`). Don't retire a converting angle because *you're* tired of it.
- **Ignore the platform's "optimize your account" suggestions.** They serve the platform's revenue. Trust your own cost per lead and cost per deal (`wv3NxhGSA2w`).
- **Scale a winner by cloning it, not just raising its budget** (`qHA2c15T58w`). Change one true word or audience cue (for example, add "for landlords") so the algorithm finds a new pocket. Match the landing page headline to the winning ad's exact angle. Exclude existing customers and leads from cold campaigns and refresh that list regularly. At scale, Sabri uses cost caps near 80% of the maximum you can pay per customer (his method).
- **Allowable cost per lead ≈ customer value × lead-to-customer rate** (Georgi, `igQnL-O4ipw`). For example, a $1,000 sale at a 1% lead-to-customer rate supports about $10 per lead.

## 9. Get feedback from buyers, not friends
Show drafts to people in the target market. Friends, receptionists and "normies" judge length and tone wrongly, because they were never going to buy (Sabri). Check reading level with the Hemingway App and aim for grade 5 to 6.

## 10. Log what wins
Put every winner in [swipe-file.md](swipe-file.md) with the numbers, the audience, the awareness level and **one line on why it won**. The log is how the team's judgment compounds.
