# Sell fear: open on the landscape

Owner rule, 2026-10-09: **"we sell fear always,"** and every ad makes people think about the current landscape of life and real estate. It applies to every ad, every format (skeleton, crochet, singing, claymation, talking objects, UGC, game-look, statics) and every product category.

Order of authority: the [claims registry](claims-registry.md) decides which words are allowed. This file decides how the ad opens and how much of it is fear. [sell-the-destination.md](sell-the-destination.md) decides how it ends.

## The shape

| Beat | Tag | What it does | In a ~45 s cold ad |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The landscape | PAIN | 1-3 true, sourced facts about how life or the market has changed: something people would want to know anyway. Motion on screen in the first second | 0-12 s |
| 2. Her | PAIN | Zoom from the world to one person living inside those facts, in third person. The viewer thinks "that's my street" | 12-15 s |
| 3. The cost of standing still | PAIN | What waiting costs her, shown with things on screen (a spreading stain, a stack of envelopes), never adjectives. Only lines that are sourced or true by definition | 15-21 s |
| 4. The turn | PLANE | The mechanism, in one line | ~2 s |
| 5. The way out | PATH | Her easy, one-time steps, from her side | 23-29 s |
| 6. Her next chapter | DESTINATION | One concrete scene: a person, a place, an action | 29-41 s |
| 7. The Ask | PATH | A question that turns the landscape on the viewer's own house, then the registry Ask | 41-45 s |

The loops nest as usual: the landscape opens A (the master loop), her situation opens B, the cost of waiting opens C, and they close C, B, A.

## Why fear, and why it still needs a way out

- **Fear works.** Tannenbaum et al. (2015) pooled 127 articles and 248 samples (27,372 people). Fear appeals moved attitudes, intentions and behavior (d = 0.29). There were very few conditions where they failed and none where they backfired.
- **It works harder under conditions we control:**
  1. **High severity and susceptibility.** The landscape facts carry the severity. A character the viewer recognizes carries the "this could be me".
  2. **Efficacy statements.** The message shows the fix works and is doable. That's PATH (easy steps) and DESTINATION (proof it worked), so neither is optional.
  3. **A one-time behavior.** Fear moved people more when it asked for a single act than a repeated one. Selling a house is one act, so this is fear's best case.
  4. The same meta-analysis found stronger effects in audiences with more women. That's a finding, not a targeting instruction.
- **The way out is "it works and it's easy", not "it's nice".** Keep PATH to one to three easy steps from her side. A long process reads as effort.

## Rules for landscape facts

1. **True, current, sourced.** Every number comes from the fact bank below and appears with a source card on screen ("NAR, 2025"). When a source publishes a new edition, re-check the fact; one older than its latest edition is stale.
2. **About the world, never the business.** Numbers about Property Abundance stay banned (registry Section 4).
3. **Facts, not forecasts.** "Prices will drop" and "rates will rise" are predictions dressed as facts: never. Use present-tense facts, or lines true by definition ("Every year she waits, the house is a year older.").
4. **The landscape is the urgency.** No invented deadlines or scarcity (registry Section 4).
5. **The fear lands on a third-person character.** Never "you are" or "you have" plus a personal attribute (Meta's housing special ad category). The closing question asks about the thing ("What year was your house built?"), never the person.
6. **Things, not shock.** Fear comes from facts and objects on screen, not disasters, gore or crying faces. Meta rejects sensational content.
7. **No real newspaper mastheads, outlet logos or recreated headlines from real outlets.** Show facts as plain data cards with the source line.
8. **Photoreal people never pose as sellers** (registry Section 4; the owner refuses AI labels). Show the character through her hands, her house or from behind, or use a stylized format (clay, crochet, game-look).

## Any format, any category

| Format | How the landscape opens |
|---|---|
| Documentary / data | Archival-look footage with one data card per fact; the character through hands and objects |
| Skeleton | The narrator states the fact; the skeleton lives inside it |
| Crochet, claymation, story ads | The world in miniature first (a yarn street aging in a time-lapse), then the character |
| Singing | Verse one is the landscape |
| Talking objects | The object states the fact about itself ("I'm 42 years old and nobody's coming to fix me") |
| UGC presenter | The company's presenter says the fact to camera with the source card, never as a seller |
| Static | Headline = the fact, sub = her, then the Ask |
| Another product category | Build that category's fact bank first, under the same rules, then use the same shape |

## Worked example: "Three Things" (Property Abundance, cold, ~47 s + end card)

```
[OPEN A]  PAIN   Three things are happening to American houses.
          WHAT WE SEE: Archival-look 1980s footage, moving from the first frame: a crew raises a wall on a
          brand-new street while kids race bikes past. The street ages in a fast time-lapse: paint fades,
          trees grow, gutters sag.
[payload] PAIN   They're getting older. The typical one is now 42.
          WHAT WE SEE: Hard cut to one of those houses today, same angle.
          Card: 42 YEARS · Median age of owner-occupied homes · NAHB, 2026
[payload] PAIN   They cost more to keep. Insurance is up 24% in three years.
          WHAT WE SEE: An insurance envelope drops through a mail slot onto a pile of others.
          Card: +24% · Typical homeowner premium, 2021-2024 · Consumer Federation of America
[payload] PAIN   And first-time buyers are the smallest share on record.
          WHAT WE SEE: An open house, front door wide, cookies on the counter. Nobody comes.
          Card: 21% · First-time buyers' share, lowest on record · NAR, 2025
[OPEN B]  PAIN   Now take her. Same roof since the eighties.
          WHAT WE SEE: Inside one of those houses. Pencil height marks climb a doorframe. A woman's hand
          in her sixties grips the banister; we see her from behind as she climbs, slowly.
[payload] PAIN   Every year she waits, the house is a year older.
          WHAT WE SEE: Locked-camera time-lapse in her kitchen: seasons wheel past the window, a ceiling
          stain spreads, envelopes stack up on the table.
[OPEN C]  PAIN   Fix it to sell it? Or keep paying to keep it?
          WHAT WE SEE: Her hand drops a contractor's estimate (blank pages) onto a stack of others.
[CLOSE C] PLANE  Neither. The house sells as-is.
          WHAT WE SEE: Her hand stops above the stack. Pill: SELLS AS-IS
[payload] PATH   She makes one call.
          WHAT WE SEE: Phone to her ear, she looks up from the stack.
[payload] PATH   Takes what matters, leaves the rest, picks her closing date.
          WHAT WE SEE: She lifts one framed photo off the wall; the boxes stay. She circles a date on
          the kitchen calendar.
[CLOSE B] DEST   Now Sunday dinner's at her place. One floor. Ten minutes from the grandkids.
          WHAT WE SEE: Her new ground-floor kitchen. The grandkids burst through the door; she laughs at
          the stove.
[payload] DEST   Saturdays are soccer games, not hardware stores.
          WHAT WE SEE: Bleachers. She jumps up cheering, juice box in hand.
[CLOSE A] DEST   The old house turns 43 without her. Her new doorframe already has its first pencil mark.
          WHAT WE SEE: One beat on the old house, empty porch, leaves blowing. Then her granddaughter
          stands against the new doorframe while she pencils the first height mark.
[CTA]     PATH   What year was your house built? Ask for a cash offer. No obligation.
          WHAT WE SEE: End card: CASH OFFER · Sells as-is · No commission · No closing costs · You pick
          the closing date. "No obligation." in yellow. Holds 2.5 s.

TAGS (134 spoken words, ~47 s at 2.85 words/s): PAIN 66 (49%) · PLANE 5 (4%) · PATH 27 (20%) · DESTINATION 36 (27%)
```

Why it's built this way: the three landscape facts open the master loop ("three things") and the last line closes it ("turns 43 without her"). "Every year she waits, the house is a year older" carries the fear and is true by definition. The doorframe pencil marks open on the old house and close on the new one. The Ask turns the landscape on the viewer's own house.
