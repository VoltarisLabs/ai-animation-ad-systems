# Property Abundance — Psychological Profile & Hook Map

**Layer:** deep psychology on top of `README.md` (the three primary avatars).
**Purpose:** give a hook writer the emotional mechanics behind each avatar, so the first 3 seconds hit the real driver instead of the stated one.
**Sources:** `04_Audience_Research/Research_Document_Cash_Home_Buying.md` (17 Reddit Answers responses, 170 source threads, collected 2026-09-22) and `Buyer_Avatar/README.md`.
**Hook taxonomy:** the eight opening moves from the `hook-engine` skill (Cold Open, Two-Beat, Number Drop, Then/Now, Admission, Callout, Negative, Mid-Action).

---

## How to read this file

Every claim is labelled:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[R]** | Grounded in the research document — a Reddit quote, a pain point, an objection or a persona detail that is actually in the data. |
| **[I]** | Inference. Standard buyer psychology applied to the [R] material. Reasonable, not measured. Test it, don't quote it as fact. |

Two hard rules carried over from the research file:

1. **Reddit quotes are internal only.** They show tone and vocabulary. They are never testimonials (FTC 16 CFR 255.1). Rewrite before anything goes on screen.
2. **Offer specifics are PLACEHOLDER until the business confirms them.** 24-hour offer, 7-day close, no fees, no repairs, "we don't assign the contract", proof of funds — all still unconfirmed in the research file. Any hook below that leans on one is marked **⚠ claim-dependent**. Confirm it or cut it.

# The one psychological fact under all three avatars

None of these people are shopping for a house sale. They are trying to end a situation that is eating them. **[R]** — "This house has drained my energy, finances, health, and happiness." / "I just wanted it SOLD." / "Cut out all the bullshit."

That changes what a hook must do. A hook aimed at a shopper sells value. A hook aimed at these three must do one of two things in the first second: **name the exact situation they are stuck in**, or **name the fear that stops them acting**. Value language ("top dollar", "best offer") reads as a sales pitch and confirms the thing they already suspect — that you are one of the predators. **[R]** — "Avoid *top dollar*, *best price guaranteed*... Readers call these lies."

**The emotional order is: exhaustion > fear > money.** **[R]** — "Peace of mind matters more than squeezing out every dollar." Hooks that lead on price lose to hooks that lead on relief, for all three.

# AVATAR 1 — Emily "The Heir" Carter (Overwhelmed Inheritor)

## Core wound

**The house is her parent, and she wants to get rid of it.** **[I]** Selling feels like a second burial, and she cannot say that out loud. Every practical problem (taxes, clean-out, the drive) is carrying a grief charge it did not earn. **[R]** — "I'm worried about being sad and grieving at the same time I will have to deal with all of their logistics and bills and money and inheritance. It's crazy it all hits at once."

## Identity & self-story

- Sees herself as the responsible one — the sibling who handles things. **[I]**
- Did not ask for the role and cannot refuse it without looking like the bad child. **[I]**
- Her competence is on trial in front of her family. A bad sale is a public failure of that role. **[R]** — "My sibling doesn't want to put any money into it but refuses to consider a cash offer."

## Dominant emotion and what it does to attention

**Overwhelm.** **[R]** Overwhelm narrows attention to whatever removes a task. **[I]** She does not read carefully; she scans for "is this one more thing, or one less thing?" A hook with a list of steps loses. A hook that removes something wins.

## Fear ladder

| Level | Fear | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Getting a lowball price because she's in a hurry | [R] |
| Real | The clean-out — going through a dead parent's belongings alone | [R] |
| Deepest | Being blamed by her family for selling the family home too cheap, and being the one who "let it go" | [I], family-conflict pain is [R] |

## Stated vs hidden desire

- **Stated:** a simple sale, no repairs, no travel. **[R]**
- **Hidden:** permission to be done. She wants an outside authority to make "sell it fast and cheap" the sensible adult choice rather than the lazy one. **[I]**

That is the single biggest hook opportunity on this avatar. **Absolution, not persuasion.**

## Beliefs & worldview

- "Selling the house is what I'm supposed to avoid until everything is sorted." **[I]**
- "Cash buyers pay below market — that's how they make money." **[R]** She already believes this. Arguing with it fails; naming it first works.
- "Renting it out is the responsible option" — and privately she dreads it. **[R]** — "managing tenants from this far away... could just turn into another thing on my plate lol."
- "A vacant house is a problem that can lead to bigger problems." **[R]** Latent urgency she has already voiced to herself.

## Emotional contradictions (why she stalls)

1. Wants it gone / feels guilty wanting it gone. **[I]**
2. Wants speed / fears speed means she got played. **[R]**
3. Wants family agreement / knows the family will never fully agree. **[R]**
4. Won't spend money to fix it / won't accept the discount for not fixing it. **[I]**

A hook that names a contradiction out loud creates instant recognition. She has never heard anyone say it.

## Awareness stage (Schwartz)

**Problem-aware, solution-skeptical.** **[I]** She knows the house is a problem and knows cash buyers exist. She does not believe they are safe. So the hook's job is not education — it is **disarming**.

## Trigger moments — the exact seconds she becomes reachable

- The property tax or insurance bill lands on a house she does not live in. **[R]**
- She gets back from another round trip "for stupid stuff." **[R]**
- She opens the closet with her parent's clothes still in it. **[I]**
- A sibling says no to something for the third time. **[R]**
- The realtor drops the price again. **[R]**

Write hooks that land inside one of these moments, not in the abstract.

## Objections decoded

| What she says [R] | What it actually means [I] | What disarms it |
|---|---|---|
| "Am I getting ripped off because I just want this house gone?" | "Is my exhaustion being priced in against me?" | Show the math before the number. The explained offer is the trust lever. |
| "Are you a wholesaler?" | "Will I be handed off to a stranger?" | A plain sentence about who actually signs — ⚠ claim-dependent. |
| "Why is your offer lower than Zillow?" | "I need a defensible reason to tell my family." | Give her the argument she can repeat at the family table. |
| "What if my family disagrees?" | "I can't be the one who decided." | Position it as information the family can look at together, not a decision she makes alone. |

## Her vocabulary (internal reference — rewrite before use)

as-is · clean-out · probate · title · "driving back for stupid stuff" · "another thing on my plate" · "just want to be done with it" · hoarder house · the estate · comps · Zestimate **[R]**

## Hook levers for Emily

1. **Absolution** — make stopping the responsible choice.
2. **Task subtraction** — every hook removes something (the drive, the clean-out, the sorting).
3. **The unspoken sentence** — say what she thinks and won't admit.
4. **Family-blame shield** — give her the line she can hand to her siblings.
5. **Grief-adjacent, never grief-exploiting** — acknowledge, do not perform sympathy.
6. **Distance** — "you live hours away" is instant self-identification. **[R]**

## Hooks — Emily

*Format: `MOVE | LEVER` then the line. S = spoken open, OS = on-screen text.*

1. `Callout | Distance` — **OS:** "You inherited a house three hours away."
2. `Cold Open | Unspoken sentence` — **S:** "You're allowed to not want the house."
3. `Negative | Absolution` — **S:** "Keeping it is not honoring them. It's just expensive."
4. `Two-Beat | Task subtraction` — **OS:** "You don't have to clean it out." / "Not one box."
5. `Mid-Action | Trigger moment` — **S:** "So that's the fourth trip this month, for a house you don't live in."
6. `Admission | Disarm` — **S:** "Yeah, we're one of those cash buyers. Here's how the number is actually built." ⚠ claim-dependent (must then show the math)
7. `Callout | Family shield` — **OS:** "Your sibling won't pay for repairs and won't take an offer."
8. `Negative | Absolution` — **OS:** "Renting it out is not the safe option. It's a second job."
9. `Cold Open | Unspoken sentence` — **S:** "Nobody tells you the estate paperwork starts before the grief ends."
10. `Two-Beat | Latent urgency` — **OS:** "An empty house doesn't stay empty." / "It just stops being yours first."
11. `Callout | Task subtraction` — **S:** "If the closet is still full and you can't face it, this is for you."
12. `Then/Now | Relief picture` — **OS:** "March: still paying taxes on it." / "April: not your problem." ⚠ claim-dependent (timeline)

## Hook killers for Emily

- Any hype urgency ("act now", "limited"). She reads it as predation. **[R]**
- Sympathy theatre ("we know how hard it is to lose a parent"). Feels like a script. **[I]**
- Price-led openers. Confirms her lowball fear before you get a second of trust. **[R]**
- Multi-step process in the hook. Adds a task to someone whose whole motive is fewer tasks. **[I]**

# AVATAR 2 — Marcus "The Cornered Homeowner" Davis (Financially Cornered)

## Core wound

**Shame.** **[R]** — the persona notes "letters from the lender, fear, and shame." He is not primarily afraid of losing money. He is afraid of being seen as a man who lost the house. **[I]** Everything else sits on top of that.

## Identity & self-story

- Provider. The house is proof he provides. **[I]**
- He believes this happened *to* him — a job loss, an event knocked him off track. **[R]** — "a job loss or life event knocked him off track."
- He is doing his research alone, at night, so nobody knows. **[R]** — "Calling his servicer, searching his options late at night."

## Dominant emotion and what it does to attention

**Anxiety under a lid.** **[I]** Anxiety makes attention hyper-alert to threat and to authority. He is scanning for two things simultaneously: *is there a way out* and *is this person about to take advantage of me*. That double scan is why he bounces off confident sales language — confidence reads as a closer. **[R]** — "They focus on the three Ds: death, divorce, destitution."

## Fear ladder

| Level | Fear | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Getting lowballed | [R] |
| Real | Running out of time — "the longer you wait the less options you have" | [R] |
| Deepest | His family finding out how bad it got, and losing the equity *and* the credit, ending up with nothing to show | [I], equity/credit fear is [R] |

## Stated vs hidden desire

- **Stated:** a clear timeline, a fair offer, avoid foreclosure. **[R]**
- **Hidden:** to stop being the person who has to decide. He wants someone competent and non-judgmental to lay the options on the table so the decision makes itself. **[I]** — his stated interest is literally "a clear timeline and a straight answer." **[R]**

## Beliefs & worldview

- "Cash buyers hunt people like me." **[R]**
- "If I admit I'm behind, my leverage disappears." **[I]**
- "The clock is the enemy." **[R]**
- "Proof of funds letters are often drawn on some vague LLC." **[R]** — he has specific, technical distrust, not vague distrust. He knows the vocabulary of the scam.

That last point matters: **Marcus cannot be beaten on emotion, only on specificity.** He respects operational detail because detail is the thing scammers skip. **[I]**

## Emotional contradictions (why he stalls)

1. Needs urgency / recoils from anyone who creates urgency. **[I]**
2. Wants someone to tell him what to do / will not be told what to do. **[I]**
3. Wants the problem gone / selling admits the failure, so delay feels like hope. **[I]**
4. Hides the situation / cannot get help without disclosing it. **[I]**

Contradiction 3 is the real reason he does nothing until the options are almost gone.

## Awareness stage

**Problem-aware, urgently. Solution-aware but threat-flagged.** **[I]** He does not need the problem explained. He needs a route that does not feel like a trap, and he needs it told calmly.

## Trigger moments

- A certified letter from the servicer. **[R]**
- 2 a.m. searching — "can my house get foreclosed after being 3 months behind?" **[R]**
- The moment he calculates how many months of savings are left. **[R]** — "I can probably manage for another two months at most."
- A notice with a date on it. **[I]**

## Objections decoded

| What he says [R] | What it actually means [I] | What disarms it |
|---|---|---|
| "You're trying to take advantage of me because you know I'm in trouble." | "I need to know you profit without me losing." | Say the margin out loud. Explaining how you make money is the most disarming move available. |
| "Do you have the money? Proof of funds?" | "Prove you're not a paper LLC." | Operational specifics — ⚠ claim-dependent. |
| "Will you change the price later?" | "I can't survive a restart." **[R]** — "2 months later they offered 10% less... by that time I didn't want to start over." | Name the late price drop before he does. |
| "What happens if I say no?" | "Am I trapped once I talk to you?" | Explicit exit. No-obligation, stated plainly. ⚠ claim-dependent. |

## His vocabulary (internal reference)

behind on payments · underwater · equity · earnest money · proof of funds · "and assigns" · servicer · comps · lowball · credit · options **[R]**

## Hook levers for Marcus

1. **Calm** — a lower emotional temperature than the situation. Calm reads as competence.
2. **Time, stated as fact, never as pressure** — "here's what happens at day 120", not "act now".
3. **Self-incrimination** — admit the downside of your own offer first. The research says distrust is the main barrier; volunteering the negative is the fastest credibility purchase available. **[R]/[I]**
4. **Option-mapping, not selling** — including options that are not you (HUD counselor, loan mod). **[R]** — the research explicitly recommends pointing to housing counselors.
5. **Privacy** — the hook should imply discretion. **[I]**
6. **Non-judgment** — never the word "distressed". Never "desperate".

## Hooks — Marcus

1. `Admission | Self-incrimination` — **S:** "A cash offer is less than a listed sale. Here's when it still wins."
2. `Cold Open | Non-judgment` — **S:** "Being behind is not the same as being out of options."
3. `Negative | Calm` — **S:** "Foreclosure is not the deadline everyone thinks it is."
4. `Callout | Trigger moment` — **OS:** "You looked this up at 2am."
5. `Number Drop | Option-mapping` — **S:** "Three months behind. Four things can still happen. Only one of them is selling." ⚠ verify the four before shooting
6. `Two-Beat | Self-incrimination` — **OS:** "We make money on this." / "Here's exactly how much."
7. `Negative | Privacy` — **OS:** "Nobody has to know the house is in trouble."
8. `Mid-Action | Fear naming` — **S:** "So the offer came in, and then two months later it dropped ten percent. That's the part nobody warns you about."
9. `Callout | Non-judgment` — **S:** "If you're paying the mortgage out of savings and counting how many months are left, keep watching."
10. `Cold Open | Option-mapping` — **S:** "Before you call anyone who buys houses, call a HUD counselor. Free. Here's why."
11. `Negative | Calm` — **OS:** "You don't have to decide today. You do have to know the dates."
12. `Two-Beat | Equity` — **OS:** "The house isn't the thing you lose first." / "The equity is."

## Hook killers for Marcus

- Countdowns, "before it's too late", any manufactured scarcity. **[R]** — the research explicitly warns against urgency hype that preys on distress.
- The words *distressed*, *desperate*, *struggling*, *facing foreclosure* used at him. Labels the shame. **[I]**
- Big promises with no mechanism. He is trained to spot the vague LLC. **[R]**
- Overly warm, high-energy delivery. Reads as a closer. **[I]**

# AVATAR 3 — Jason "The Burned-Out Landlord" Miller

## Core wound

**Humiliation by a system he thought he was smart enough to work.** **[R]** — "I honestly feel like I'm a victim and the tenant is a bully here." He bought an asset and it made him the mark. **[I]** The financial loss hurts less than the powerlessness.

## Identity & self-story

- The one who invested instead of just working. **[R]** — side investor with a day job.
- Sees himself as rational and fair — and the situation has made him feel neither. **[R]** — values "fairness, his time, his financial safety."
- Private embarrassment: he told people it was a good investment. **[I]**

## Dominant emotion and what it does to attention

**Anger, gone flat into exhaustion.** **[R]** — "Bleeding money for months on an attorney is gutting me." Anger shortens patience to near zero. **[I]** He will not sit through setup. He wants a number and an exit. **[R]** — "Just give me a number and let me decide."

Of the three, Jason has the **shortest hook window and the lowest tolerance for anything that sounds like a process.**

## Fear ladder

| Level | Fear | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | "Nobody buys with a problem tenant inside." | [R] |
| Real | This drags another six months and costs more in fees than the loss so far | [R] |
| Deepest | That he made a permanent financial mistake and there is no clean exit — only a slower bleed | [I] |

## Stated vs hidden desire

- **Stated:** sell it, stop the bleed, stop dealing with tenants. **[R]**
- **Hidden:** to be told he is not stupid, and that the loss stops being his the day he signs. **[I]** A clean transfer of the problem is worth more to him than a higher number. He will trade dollars for finality — and he knows it, which is its own irritation.

## Beliefs & worldview

- "The system protects bad tenants." **[R]**
- "Every buyer will run the second I say 'occupied'." **[R]**
- "Anyone who says they can close fast is about to renegotiate." **[R]**
- "Cutting my losses fast is the adult move." **[R]** — he already agrees with selling. The question is only whether you are real.

**Jason is the closest to ready of the three.** **[I]** He does not need convincing that selling is right. He needs proof that the specific obstacle — the tenant — is not a deal breaker.

## Emotional contradictions (why he stalls)

1. Wants out now / refuses to "let the tenant win" by taking a loss. **[I]**
2. Says it's only money / it is clearly not only money. **[I]**
3. Hates the process / keeps choosing the longest process (court). **[R]**
4. Wants a fast number / will distrust a number that comes fast. **[I]**

## Awareness stage

**Solution-aware, most-aware on a good day.** **[I]** He has already considered cash buyers. He has probably already been burned by or warned about a wholesaler. **[R]** The hook's job is **qualification** — prove you buy occupied, or he is gone in one second.

## Trigger moments

- The eviction hearing gets continued again. **[R]**
- The attorney invoice arrives. **[R]**
- Rent day passes with nothing. **[R]**
- He walks the property and sees new damage. **[R]**

## Objections decoded

| What he says [R] | What it actually means [I] | What disarms it |
|---|---|---|
| "You're not actually going to buy it with the tenant still there." | "Don't waste my time — confirm it in the first line." | Say "occupied" in the hook itself. ⚠ claim-dependent: only if true. |
| "Are you going to renegotiate later?" | "I've already lost months. I can't lose more." | Pre-empt the renegotiation fear before he raises it. |
| "Are you a wholesaler?" | "Am I about to be shopped around?" | Plain answer. ⚠ claim-dependent. |
| "How far below market?" | "Tell me the real number so I can stop thinking." | Lead with the mechanism, then the number. Never dodge it. |

## His vocabulary (internal reference)

eviction · cash for keys · bleeding money · attorney fees · non-paying tenant · occupied · turn · month-to-month · court date · cut my losses **[R]**

## Hook levers for Jason

1. **Qualification in the first second** — the word *occupied* or *tenant still in it* is the hook.
2. **Arithmetic of the bleed** — he thinks in monthly loss. Speak in it.
3. **Finality** — the promise is not money, it's "it stops being yours."
4. **Anti-wholesaler positioning** — the one credential he cares about. ⚠ claim-dependent.
5. **No process language** — "quick form", "we'll walk you through it" kills it. **[I]**
6. **Permission to stop** — reframe cutting losses as the competent move, not surrender.

## Hooks — Jason

1. `Callout | Qualification` — **OS:** "Tenant still in it? Still buying."  ⚠ claim-dependent
2. `Cold Open | Finality` — **S:** "You can stop paying a mortgage on a house that doesn't pay you."
3. `Number Drop | Arithmetic` — **S:** "Four months of attorney fees costs more than the rent you're chasing." ⚠ verify before using a specific figure
4. `Negative | Permission to stop` — **S:** "Winning the eviction is not the same as winning."
5. `Mid-Action | Trigger moment` — **S:** "So the hearing got pushed again. Third time."
6. `Two-Beat | Finality` — **OS:** "The tenant stays." / "The problem doesn't stay yours."  ⚠ claim-dependent
7. `Admission | Anti-wholesaler` — **S:** "Most people who call you about this don't have the money. Ask them one question." ⚠ claim-dependent on your own answer
8. `Callout | Arithmetic` — **OS:** "You're covering the mortgage out of your own paycheck."
9. `Negative | Permission to stop` — **OS:** "It stopped being an investment months ago."
10. `Cold Open | No process` — **S:** "You want a number, not a process. Here's how the number is built."
11. `Two-Beat | Renegotiation fear` — **OS:** "The price at the start." / "That's the fear, right?"
12. `Mid-Action | Bleed` — **S:** "Rent day was Tuesday. Nothing came in. Again."

## Hook killers for Jason

- Anything that sounds like a form, a process, or a walkthrough. **[I]**
- Sympathy. He wants competence, not comfort. **[I]**
- Hedging on the tenant question. One second of ambiguity and he's gone. **[I]**
- Talking about the house's potential. He has heard it. **[I]**

# Cross-avatar hook doctrine

## The three gates every hook must pass

1. **Situation gate** — can the right person recognise themselves in under one second? If the hook could belong to any homeowner, it belongs to none of them.
2. **Predator gate** — could this line have been written by the scammer they already fear? If yes, rewrite. This kills every urgency, scarcity and "top dollar" line. **[R]**
3. **Truth gate** — does the line assert anything not confirmed by the business? Every PLACEHOLDER in the research file (24-hour offer, 7-day close, no fees, no repairs, direct purchase, proof of funds) is unconfirmed. **[R]** No number, timeline or guarantee on screen unless it is verified.

## Which lever wins, per avatar

| | Emily | Marcus | Jason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant emotion | Overwhelm | Shame under anxiety | Flattened anger |
| Core wound | Grief disguised as logistics | Being seen as the man who lost the house | Being made a mark by a system |
| What she/he actually buys | Permission to stop | A calm map of the options | Finality, today |
| Hook must deliver | Absolution + task removal | Calm + self-incriminating honesty | Qualification + arithmetic |
| Strongest opening moves | Callout, Negative, Two-Beat | Admission, Cold Open, Negative | Callout, Number Drop, Mid-Action |
| Hook window | Short (scanning) | Medium (reading carefully for threat) | Shortest (zero patience) |
| Fastest kill | Hype urgency | A label ("distressed") | Process language |

## Universal banned language

From the research, verbatim guidance: avoid *top dollar*, *best price guaranteed*, *we pay more than anyone* — "Readers call these lies." **[R]**

Add, by inference from the objection set **[I]**: *distressed*, *desperate*, *motivated seller*, *act now*, *limited time*, *don't miss out*, *hassle-free* (hollow), *we care about you*, any countdown, any stock-photo handshake energy.

## The one move that outperforms on all three

**Self-incrimination.** Say the bad part about your own offer before they think it. "A cash offer is lower than a full retail sale after repairs — here's the math, and here's what you actually walk away with." The research names distrust as the main barrier **[R]**, and this is the cheapest way to buy trust in three seconds. **[I]**

## Hook scoring rubric (use before shooting)

Score each hook 0–2 on five axes. Under 7, rewrite.

| Axis | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-recognition | Any homeowner | The right category | The exact moment |
| Emotional precision | Generic stress | Right emotion | The unspoken sentence |
| Trust posture | Sounds like a pitch | Neutral | Volunteers a downside |
| Specificity | Vague | One detail | A detail only an insider knows |
| Truth | Asserts a PLACEHOLDER | Vague enough to be safe | Verified or claim-free |
