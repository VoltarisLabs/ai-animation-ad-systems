## Brian Dean (Backlinko)

**Frameworks**
- **APP intro formula.** **A**gree (name a pain the reader already feels) -> **P**romise (the result) -> **P**review (what's coming).
- **Bucket brigades.** Short transition lines that keep people scrolling: "Here's the deal:", "Truth is:", "But wait...". This is an old copywriting device that Dean popularized for SEO.
- **Skyscraper Technique.** Find content that already ranks and earns links. Beat it on depth, freshness and design. Then reach out to the sites linking to the original.

**Rules**
- Keep intros very short. Get to the promise fast.
- Place bucket brigades where readers tend to drop off.
- Match search intent. Format the page for how the searcher wants the answer.

**Triggers:** Agreement and rapport, curiosity gaps, a promised outcome.
**Example:** Backlinko reports that its first Skyscraper post more than doubled traffic (+110%) in about two weeks.
**Sources:** backlinko.com/skyscraper-technique ; copyhackers.com/engage-bucket-brigade-technique/ ; ahrefs.com/blog/skyscraper-technique/
**Confidence:** High on Skyscraper. APP and bucket brigades are confirmed by secondary sources.

**Paid ad use:** APP works as a 3-line ad opener: "Tired of X? Here's how to get Y. In the next 30 seconds: Z."

## Neil Patel

**Frameworks**
- **4-Point Content Framework:** (1) Compelling headline and intro, (2) formatting and media (subheads, images, whitespace), (3) cohesive storytelling, (4) quality refinement (editing, readability tools, quotable lines).
- Beyond this he has no widely recognized proprietary formula. He is known mainly for tools (Ubersuggest) and high-volume tactical content. Other "Neil Patel frameworks" are **unverified**.

**Rules:** Readability first. Curiosity headlines. Format for skimmers.
**Source:** neilpatel.com/blog/the-4-point-content-framework-thatll-hook-your-readers-and-double-your-time-on-site/
**Confidence:** Medium.
