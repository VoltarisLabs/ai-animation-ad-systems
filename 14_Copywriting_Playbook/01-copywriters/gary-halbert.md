# Gary Halbert

**Who:** Direct-mail copywriter known as the "Prince of Print." His long-form sales letters are credited with very large sales.
**Key works:** *The Boron Letters* (letters to his son Bond, written in the 1980s), *The Gary Halbert Letter* newsletter.
**Core idea:** Who you sell to matters more than what you say. Find a starving crowd first.

## 1. Frameworks
- **The Starving Crowd.** Halbert's classic classroom question: if you opened a hamburger stand and could have one advantage, what would it be? His answer was not a better recipe or location. It was a starving crowd. The market's hunger beats the quality of the copy.
- **Market -> Offer -> Copy.** Choose a responsive list first, then an irresistible offer, and only then write.
- **A-pile vs. B-pile.** People sort mail into personal letters (A-pile, opened) and obvious ads (B-pile, often thrown away). Make your piece look like it belongs in the A-pile.
- **AIDA**, applied hard: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

## 2. Actionable rules
1. Study your market's buying history: people who already bought something similar are the best prospects.
2. Copy great ads out by hand to absorb their rhythm and structure.
3. Short words, short sentences, short paragraphs. Make the copy easy to read.
4. Write the way people talk, to one reader.
5. Make the offer so good it feels foolish to say no. Offer beats wording.
6. Give a reason why: explain why the price or deal exists.
7. Admit a weakness early (the "damaging admission") to buy credibility for the rest.
8. Tell a story. People read stories when they would skip claims.
9. Edit ruthlessly. Draft fast, then cut.

## 3. Psychological triggers
Curiosity, personal relevance and self-interest, candor (damaging admission), reason-why, novelty, strong risk reversal.

## 4. Examples
- **Coat-of-arms letter:** A personal-looking letter that sold family crest reports by surname. Personalization plus pride in family identity made it one of the most mailed letters in history.
- **"Dollar bill" letter:** A real dollar attached to the letter. The object guaranteed attention and created a sense of reciprocity before the pitch.

## Apply it
Before writing any ad, pick the hungriest audience you can find: people already searching, already comparing, already in pain.
