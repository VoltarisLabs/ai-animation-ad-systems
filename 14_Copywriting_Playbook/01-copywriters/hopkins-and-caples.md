# Claude Hopkins and John Caples

Early pioneers who treated advertising as something you measure, not something you guess at.

---

## Claude C. Hopkins
**Key works:** *Scientific Advertising* (1923), *My Life in Advertising* (1927). Worked at Lord & Thomas.
**Core idea:** Advertising is salesmanship. If a salesman would not say it, the ad should not say it. Measure every ad by what it sells. (The phrase "salesmanship in print" is usually credited to his colleague John E. Kennedy.)

### Frameworks
- **Keyed ads and coupons.** Each ad carried a code or coupon so response could be traced to it. This was an early form of conversion tracking.
- **Test small, then scale.** Run an ad in a small market, measure the cost per sale, and only then roll it out.
- **The preemptive claim.** Describe a process the whole industry uses that no one had advertised, and it becomes yours.
- **Sampling.** Let people try the product. Free samples made the ad pay for itself.

### Actionable rules
1. Be specific. Vague claims are ignored. Exact claims are believed.
2. Sell service and benefit, not the product. Tell people what they get.
3. Headlines should pick out the people who will buy.
4. Do not try to be amusing. People do not buy from clowns.
5. Measure cost per customer, not impressions.

### Examples
- **Schlitz beer:** Hopkins toured the brewery and found bottles were cleaned with live steam. Every brewer did this, but no one had said so. Advertising it made Schlitz own "purity."
- **Pepsodent:** Built the ad around removing the "film" on teeth, giving people a new, visible reason to brush.

---

## John Caples
**Key works:** *Tested Advertising Methods* (1932). Worked at BBDO.
**Core idea:** Test headlines, because they cause most of the difference in results.

### Frameworks
- **Headline split tests.** He ran the same ad with different headlines and saw results differ by multiples, from the headline alone.
- **Headline categories that worked:** self-interest (best), news, and curiosity *combined* with self-interest. Curiosity by itself tested weak.
- **Proven headline types:** "How to...", "Who else wants...", "Here's a quick way to...", "Are you...?", news announcements, testimonial headlines, numbered headlines.

### Actionable rules
1. Put self-interest in every headline.
2. Only use curiosity when it is tied to a benefit.
3. Name the audience in the headline when you can.
4. Simple, direct language beats clever wording in tests.
5. Keep testing after you find a winner, because the next one may beat it.

### Example
- **"They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano But When I Started to Play!" (1926, U.S. School of Music).** A story headline with an underdog reversal and social payoff. It has been copied more than almost any headline ever written. Lesson: combine an emotional story, social proof and transformation.

## Apply it (paid ads)
Every modern ad platform is Hopkins and Caples at scale: track each ad, test headlines first, and scale only on cost per customer.
