# Dan Ariely

**Who:** Behavioral economist, professor at Duke University.
**Key works:** *Predictably Irrational* (2008), *The Upside of Irrationality* (2010).
**Core idea:** People are irrational in systematic ways, so their choices can be predicted and designed for.

## 1. Frameworks
- **Relativity and the decoy effect.** People judge options by comparing them to nearby options. A deliberately worse option (the decoy) makes the target option look like a clear win.
- **The power of FREE.** A price of zero is treated as a category of its own, not just a low price. It pulls choice far more than the price difference would predict.
- **Arbitrary coherence.** A random first number (an anchor) can set willingness to pay. After that, later choices stay consistent with it.
- **Social norms vs. market norms.** Mixing a favor-based relationship with money can destroy goodwill.
- **IKEA effect** (Norton, Mochon & Ariely, 2012). People value things more when they helped build them.

## 2. Actionable rules
1. Offer three tiers. Make the middle or top tier the target and add a decoy that makes it look like the best deal.
2. Use "free" for the entry step (free inspection, free estimate). It outperforms "$1" by far more than a dollar should.
3. Show a reference price before your price.
4. Let prospects configure or customize (quiz, builder). Participation raises how much they value the result.
5. Don't mix social and money framing clumsily. "As a favor, here's a discount" can backfire.

## 3. Key studies
- **The Economist subscriptions:** With web-only, print-only and print+web (print and print+web at the same price), most MIT students picked print+web. Removing the useless print-only decoy shifted most of them to web-only.
- **Hershey's Kiss vs. Lindt truffle:** Dropping both prices by one cent, so the Kiss became free, flipped most people to the Kiss.
- **Social Security digits:** Students' last two SSN digits correlated with what they bid on products in an auction.

## Evidence note (important)
A 2012 paper Ariely co-authored (signing an honesty pledge at the top of a form) was retracted in 2021 after analysis showed its field data had been fabricated. Ariely has denied fabricating the data. The decoy and zero-price effects have independent support in other research, but treat single Ariely studies as hypotheses and **test before relying on them**.
