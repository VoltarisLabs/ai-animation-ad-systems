# BJ Fogg

**Who:** Founder of the Behavior Design Lab at Stanford University.
**Key works:** *Persuasive Technology* (2003), *Tiny Habits* (2019), the Fogg Behavior Model.
**Core idea:** A behavior happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt come together at the same moment.

## 1. Framework: B = MAP
- **Motivation:** How much the person wants to do it. Core motivators come in pairs: pleasure/pain, hope/fear, social acceptance/rejection.
- **Ability:** How easy it is. The limits: time, money, physical effort, mental effort, and fit with the person's routine. The scarcest one is what matters.
- **Prompt:** The cue to act now. Three kinds:
  - **Spark:** Adds motivation (for someone who can but does not want to).
  - **Facilitator:** Makes it easier (for someone who wants to but finds it hard).
  - **Signal:** A simple reminder (for someone who wants to and can).
- **The action line.** High motivation lets people do hard things. Low motivation only produces easy things. So when you cannot raise motivation, make the action easier.

## 2. Actionable rules
1. When conversions are low, reduce friction before rewriting the copy. Ability is usually the cheaper lever.
2. Match the prompt to the audience. Cold audiences need sparks. Warm audiences need facilitators. Buyers who are ready need a clear signal.
3. Make the CTA the smallest possible step: one tap, one question, "get my price."
4. Remove anything that costs time or thought at the point of action (long forms, account creation, surprise costs).
5. Put the prompt where motivation peaks: right after the proof, not before it.

## 3. Triggers
Pleasure/pain, hope/fear, acceptance/rejection, simplicity, well-timed prompts.

## 4. Example
- A lead form going from 9 fields to 3 raises ability, often with no change to motivation. Adding "takes 30 seconds" to the button is a facilitator prompt.
